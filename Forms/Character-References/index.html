My housing story

✦

Hi, my legal name is Andrew Blake-Newton, but I go by Will Saville. My former spouse is my ex, our divorce is still pending, and that is part of why my legal name change has not happened yet.

I put this together because I keep running into the same problem with housing: a screening report sees a thin rental file and poor credit, but it does not know how I got there. I am asking friends, family, former neighbors, former landlords, and other people who know me to give housing providers a fuller picture of who I am and how I actually live.

I aged out of foster care without stable housing and first applied for Section 8 in 2010. Homelessness meant moving when I had to in order to survive, and every move made it harder to stay connected to one place, one housing authority, and one waiting list. My then-spouse received a voucher in 2021 while we were legally separated but both still living in Michigan. We got back together in early 2023. After he was incarcerated near the end of 2023, the voucher stayed with our assisted household and transferred to me as the remaining spouse and household member. By 2024, I was the continuing voucher participant. It took a very long time for stable housing to stop being something I was chasing and become something I actually had.

That is why my rental and credit history looks thin on paper. A lot of the years another person might have spent building a normal lease history were years I was trying to survive homelessness and get housed. I was not avoiding stable housing. I spent years trying to reach it, and once I finally had it, I worked hard to keep it.

On September 22, 2026, Jasper and I were declined for an apartment we genuinely loved after spending $75 on the application, which was money we did not really have to spare. The screening report listed rental history that did not meet the property's requirements, insufficient income compared with the full listed rent, and limited credit experience. At the same time, that same report recorded rental history and nearly two years at my current address. That denial is what pushed me to put all of this information in one place instead of continuing to hope a screening score would explain my life for me.

One thing I do want to be clear about is the income number on that report. I receive SSI, but I am not expected to pay the entire contract rent from SSI alone. My tenant portion is calculated through the voucher program, and the housing authority separately pays a Housing Assistance Payment directly to the landlord under a HAP contract. HUD describes the family portion as usually about 30 percent of adjusted monthly income, although the amount can be higher depending on the unit and program calculation. MSHDA states that, once an approved rent is found reasonable, it contracts to pay the difference between the contract rent and the tenant portion.

I did not leave my former Michigan residence because I walked away from stable housing. I left because I was afraid staying there could put me in danger. In July 2023, I was recognized from my blog while traveling in my power wheelchair at night to get groceries near my home in Pontiac. I was shot five times with a pellet gun and subjected to anti-LGBTQ slurs. Public reporting about that attack is linked in this project.

Later, a hate group released our home address online. We were still living in the same house, down the street from where I had been recognized and attacked. Once that address was public, I had to take seriously the possibility that the same person, or someone worse, could use it to find me. We submitted documentation for a safety move out of state and were granted VAWA-related protections. My housing records include the certification packet, move approval, contract termination notice, and the later portability request for my voucher. I left because I was trying to protect the stable housing I had finally built, not because I did not value it.

After I had already left Michigan, I lost my dad. That changed what home meant to me. Jasper and I want to move back because I want to live near my mom and rebuild that part of my family life while I still can. Jasper is my fiancé and also helps me with my day-to-day care. I use a power wheelchair, so stable and accessible housing is not just a preference for us. It affects whether daily life actually works.

I can provide a former landlord reference, personal references, documentation about my housing history and the safety move, and I have offered to pay a higher security deposit if that helps a property feel more comfortable approving us. I am trying to give management enough information to judge me as a whole person, not ask anyone to ignore a legitimate concern.

If you know me personally, please do not write what you think a landlord wants to hear. Tell them what you actually know about me. A plain, honest example is more useful to me than a perfect-sounding character statement.

What the voucher means for rent and screening

✦
The voucher does not erase my credit history, but it does change how the rent is divided and who pays each part of it. That matters when an application is screened.
01

How the rent is actually paid

A Housing Choice Voucher is not extra cash handed to me. I am responsible for my tenant share, while the housing authority pays a separate Housing Assistance Payment directly to the landlord under a HAP contract. MSHDA describes that contract as its pledge to pay rent on behalf of the participant.

Sources: HUD HCV tenant guidance and MSHDA landlord guidance.

02

My share is based on income

HUD says the family rent portion is usually about 30 percent of adjusted monthly income. Depending on the unit, payment standard, utilities, and other program calculations, the family share can be higher. At initial lease-up, MSHDA will not approve a unit if the tenant would have to pay more than 40 percent of adjusted monthly income for rent and tenant-paid utilities.

Sources: HUD HCV tenant guidance and MSHDA affordability guidance.

03

The housing authority pays the landlord directly

For an approved tenancy, the housing authority and landlord sign a HAP contract that states the monthly housing assistance payment. MSHDA says its HAP payments are processed on or before the fifth business day of each month and can be sent by direct deposit.

Sources: HUD HAP contract information and MSHDA payment information.

04

My credit file and my rent coverage are two different questions

A landlord may still use lawful screening criteria, including credit-related information. I am not claiming the voucher makes poor or limited credit disappear. What it changes is how much of the contract rent has to come from my own income. Comparing the full rent only against my SSI leaves out the separate HAP that the housing authority pays directly to the owner.

Source: MSHDA tenant selection and monthly rent guidance.

05

Michigan law accounts for the subsidy in income screening

Michigan protects lawful source of income in rental housing, with a limited exemption for landlords who own five or fewer rental units. The enacted source-of-income law states that when a landlord uses a minimum income threshold, a rent voucher or subsidy must be subtracted from the monthly rent before the applicant's income is tested against that threshold. Housing Choice Vouchers and SSI are both recognized sources of income under Michigan law.

Sources: MSHDA Fair Housing and Michigan Public Act 178 of 2024, source-of-income income-threshold language. This section is informational and is not legal advice.

06

Voucher households are often long-term renters

HUD's landlord myth-busting material describes HCV tenants as typically long-term tenants, living in a unit for about 7 to 8 years on average. The same HUD material says there are no documented statistics showing HCV participants are more likely than non-HCV tenants to damage units or fail to pay rent. These program-level facts do not predict any one tenant, but they are relevant when voucher use is treated as a sign of instability.

Source: HUD HCV Myth-Busting and Benefits Fact Sheet.

07

There is a practical reason I protect the voucher

Voucher participants have to follow the lease and the program rules, pay their tenant share, and report required household or income changes. Serious violations can put the assistance at risk. After waiting so long to have stable housing, I have a very practical reason to protect both the tenancy and the voucher. That does not guarantee that any person will stay in one apartment forever, but it does matter when voucher use is treated as if it creates less incentive to remain stable.

Sources: MSHDA participant obligations and HUD tenant responsibilities.

08

Voucher waitlists can take years

HUD currently warns applicants that HCV waiting-list times can be long and depend on voucher availability and local preferences. A 2021 Center on Budget and Policy Priorities analysis of HUD data found that families who ultimately received vouchers had waited close to two and a half years nationally on average, while some of the largest housing agencies reported average waits as long as eight years. MSHDA currently opens waiting lists only when capacity is available, and lists can close because demand exceeds available assistance.

Sources: HUD waiting-list guidance, CBPP analysis of HUD data, and MSHDA waiting-list information.

Why the 156 percent number needs contextThe September 22 screening report lists $1,550 in proposed rent, $994 in monthly income, and a 156 percent rent-to-income figure. That math compares the entire contract rent with my SSI alone. My SSI is documented income, but it is not the only source used to pay rent in an HCV tenancy. The housing authority determines my tenant portion and pays the approved HAP separately to the landlord. I understand that management can still consider lawful screening criteria. I am asking that ability to pay be evaluated using the way the voucher actually works, rather than as if I personally owe the full $1,550 from SSI.
My path to a voucher took 14 yearsI first applied in 2010 and did not become the continuing voucher holder until 2024. A lot happened in those fourteen years, including homelessness, repeated moves, marriage, separation, reconciliation, and my then-spouse's incarceration. I am not saying fourteen years is a normal voucher wait. It is my history. I include it because getting stable housing took a huge part of my adult life, and I have every reason to protect the assistance that finally helped me keep it.

Screening and privacy notes

✦

Does a voucher erase poor credit?

No. My credit history is still my credit history, and a landlord may still use lawful screening criteria. My point is simply that the full contract rent is not paid from my personal income alone, so an ability-to-pay review should include the HAP that goes directly to the owner.

Is 30 percent always the exact tenant rent?

No. About 30 percent of adjusted monthly income is the usual starting point, not a promise that every tenant will pay exactly that amount. Utility allowances, deductions, payment standards, and the unit itself can change the final share. At initial lease-up, the family share generally cannot exceed 40 percent of adjusted monthly income.

Are voucher payments guaranteed forever?

No. HAP depends on an approved tenancy, continued eligibility, and compliance with the program and HAP contract. While the assistance is active, the housing authority pays its approved portion directly to the landlord.

Why include long-term tenancy data?

Because voucher use is sometimes treated as a sign that a tenant will not stay. HUD's own landlord material describes HCV households as typically long-term tenants, with an average stay of about 7 to 8 years in a unit.

Who are references for?

I may share reference statements with property managers, landlords, screening reviewers, housing authorities, housing advocates, or legal advocates when they are relevant to a housing application, reconsideration request, or appeal.

Can a response be edited later?

Yes. If you use the same browser, the site can reopen your response later as long as the saved editing access is still there. You can correct your own reference, but a submitted response cannot be deleted by the reference writer.

Should a reference writer review every document?

No. Please answer from what you personally know about me. The records and research are there for housing review; they are not a script for anyone writing a character reference.

Do I need to mention the voucher in my reference?

No. Please write about what you actually know from knowing me. The voucher information and supporting records are here for housing review, and there is no reason to repeat them unless they are personally relevant to your experience with me.
