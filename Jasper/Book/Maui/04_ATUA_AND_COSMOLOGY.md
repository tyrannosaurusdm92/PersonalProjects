# Māori atua, ancestors, kinship and cosmology

## First principle: whakapapa, not a superhero pantheon

Atua are bound to relationships, forces and genealogies. A simple 'god of X' label helps orient a newcomer but does not exhaust the figure's identity. Traditions vary among iwi, and Māori cosmologies are not merely a Māori copy of Greek mythology.

Common framework: phases named Te Kore, Te Pō and Te Ao Mārama; Ranginui and Papatūānuku are close together, and their children bring light by separating sky and earth. The weather god Tāwhirimātea resists the separation. This is a story of living relationships between land, sea, sky, ancestors and people.
Sources: https://teara.govt.nz/en/maori-creation-traditions ; https://teara.govt.nz/en/traditional-maori-religion-nga-karakia-a-te-maori/page-1

## Reference register — broad descriptions, not fixed universal portfolios

| Name | Broad traditional connections | Fiction caution |
|---|---|---|
| **Ranginui** | Sky father; parent alongside Papatūānuku | The sky's ongoing relation with earth matters as much as his 'element.' |
| **Papatūānuku** | Earth mother, land, ground of life | Not just a stone/earth elemental that heroes can summon. |
| **Tāne / Tāne Mahuta** | Forests, birds, life; separates earth and sky; human origins in some accounts | The forest is a kin relation, not background loot. |
| **Tangaroa** | Sea and marine descendants; in narratives conflicts with terrestrial family branches | Respect coastal resource relationships and risks. |
| **Tāwhirimātea** | Winds, weather and storms; opposes parental separation | Storms reflect a story of kinship and grief, not purely villainy. |
| **Tūmatauenga** | Warfare and humankind in major genealogical lines | Not a simplistic 'evil god of violence.' |
| **Rongo / Rongomātāne** | Peace and cultivated food, including kūmara | Connections to gardens, agriculture and peace-making. |
| **Haumia-tiketike** | Uncultivated food / fern root in commonly retold creation sequence | Distinguish gathered food from grown food. |
| **Rūaumoko** | Earthquakes, volcanic/underground forces in traditions | Locate a particular iwi version before dramatizing. |
| **Whiro** | Darkness, illness or destructive forces in various narratives | Context varies; avoid flattening as an all-purpose devil. |
| **Hineahuone** | First woman formed from earth in a famous genealogy | A human ancestress with profound dignity and narrative centrality. |
| **Hine-tītama / Hine-nui-te-pō** | Dawn/threshold; later great ancestress of the dead in many traditions | Her choices and agency matter; not the film's Te Kā. |
| **Mahuika** | Fire and its conservation; Māui's ancestress | She has power and anger; not a magic dispenser. |
| **Murirangawhenua** | Ancestral elder associated with the jawbone gift | Don't confuse her with Mahuika or Hina. |
| **Tamanuiterā** | The sun, caught by Māui in a major tradition | The sun has an identity, not merely a celestial prop. |
| **Hineteiwaiwa** | Pregnancy, childbirth, weaving, harvesting in published accounts | Do not conflate with Hine-nui-te-pō. |
| **Hineruhi** | Dawn, excellence in performing arts, sparkling light | Useful cultural context for performance, not an excuse to invent a rite. |
| **Tānerore** | Heat shimmer/hand trembling and origins of haka | Haka traditions are not reducible to 'war dance.' |
| **Raukatauri / Hineraukatauri** | Music, associated with case moth and pūtōrino | Music is embodied in a specific knowledge tradition. |
| **Raukatamea / Hineraukatamea** | Entertainment and performance | Distinct from Raukatauri. |
| **Hinepūtehue** | Gourd instruments, voice and reconciliation narratives | Honour the provenance of taonga pūoro. |
| **Hine-pūkohu-rangi** | Mist ancestor in Ngāi Tūhoe whakapapa | Particularly place/iwi specific; never freely reassign to a fictional family. |
| **Rēhua / Rehua** | A figure appearing in sky, warmth, seasonal or celestial traditions | Details differ among accounts; verify precise role. |

## The important women in Māui's adventures

Watch for a misleading pattern in a shallow retelling: 'a genius boy invents everything alone.' He receives life, education, materials, powers, instruction or cosmic resistance from Taranga, Murirangawhenua, Mahuika and Hine-nui-te-pō. Their decisions and knowledge form the story's architecture. The Waitangi Tribunal's Mana Wāhine evidence explicitly emphasizes the contribution of atua wāhine to Māui's feats.

Source: https://waitangitribunal.govt.nz/en/inquiries/kaupapa-inquiries/mana-wahine/te-kete-puputu/briefs-of-evidence-2/who-are-our-atua-whaea-and-what-are-their-characteristics-and-stories/key-evidence-about-atua-whaea-2

## Other legendary narrative cycles

- **Tāwhaki:** a major hero in traditions of ascent, kinship, transformation and encounter with celestial realms; the ponaturi narrative intersects some versions.
- **Tinirau, Hineteiwaiwa, Kae and Tutunui:** a whale, an abuse of trust, performance as strategy and consequential retaliation. See songs/performance sources; avoid reducing the women's group to decoration.
- **Kupe and the great octopus:** traditions of ocean voyaging and naming; distinct from Māui's fish-up feat.
- **Niwareka, Mataora and Uetonga:** the origin story given by Te Papa for tā moko, involving harm, repentance, skill and responsibility; do not lift its sacred designs for novelty tattoos.
- **Tāne and Hine:** not a romance template to copy for modern seduction; it has difficult genealogical and moral content.

## Suggested worldbuilding approach

Use this material to understand why a sea, forest, song, migration or elder is never merely an unclaimed magical object. Do **not** assign real Māori atua to govern fictional Lanternweald districts without a conscious, consulted adaptation. Invent new Lanternweald spirits and rules, clearly marked as original; if Māori people appear, let them be contemporary people rather than mythic exposition devices.
