# Maui — Film Canon, Polynesian Traditions, and Māori Cultural Research

Research date: 5 November 2026. Scope: reference library for the Maui/Jasper story **The Shore I Choose**, not a replacement for its 50 existing chapters.

## Read this first

**Three separate layers must never be silently merged:**
1. **Disney screen continuity:** Maui in the animated films Moana (2016) and Moana 2 (2024). The film's Te Fiti, Te Kā, Lalotai and narrative hook powers belong to that adaptation.
2. **Polynesian oral traditions:** Māui/Maui is a widely known culture hero/trickster in several Pacific societies, but stories, names, kinship, origins, and outcomes differ by people and place.
3. **Māori traditions of Aotearoa:** Specific Māui-tikitiki-a-Taranga accounts and the wider cosmology, performance traditions, tikanga, ancestors and beings of Māori communities. Māori traditions are living knowledge held in iwi/hapū-specific contexts, not a universal fantasy rulebook.

This folder is a research and writing resource. It does not claim religious authority, community permission to reproduce restricted knowledge, or a substitute for consultation with Māori cultural experts. Some older published retellings were filtered through colonial collecting practices. Statements are attributed to their source; variant details are labelled.

## Editorial standard

Attribute every myth to a tradition and an accessible source. Describe rather than invent karakia or whakapapa. Never make a generic 'Māori ritual dance for romance', and never transplant a Māori ancestor or taniwha into a made-up district as if it had no iwi or place relationship. Use invented Lanternweald ceremonies for Lanternweald and invite a fictional Māori performer to choose and explain their own present-day work. Do not write untranslated pseudo-te reo Māori chants. A Māori cultural reader should review any publication with detailed portrayals of living tikanga, language, sacred practices or whakapapa.
