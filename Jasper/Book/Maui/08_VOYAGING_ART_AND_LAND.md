# Ocean, voyaging, land, tattoos, arts and everyday ecology

## Voyaging is a skill, not a superpower

Moana's wayfinding milieu invites the writer to depict wind, swell, changing cloud shapes, currents, birds, stars, canoe maintenance, food provisioning and reciprocal crew labor. Specific navigation techniques differ among Pacific cultures and historical contexts. Do not casually call all Polynesian craft 'Māori waka' or collapse centuries of movement into one fantasy boat.

Useful narrative questions: Who checks the lashings? Who has the tide knowledge? How is clean water stored? How are injuries treated? How do nighttime navigation and weather judgment conflict? Who carries the knowledge when the famous hero is absent?

For a Māori-context story, a **waka** is not necessarily a single ocean-going vessel type; people distinguish craft, purposes and traditions. For a Hawaiian-context story, make room for its own voyaging language and cosmology. For a fictional Lanternweald boat, create a local design and a plausible maintenance log rather than labeling a Disney craft an authentic historical reconstruction.

Starter: https://teara.govt.nz/en/first-peoples-in-maori-tradition

## Land has relational names

- **Te Ika-a-Māui:** the great fish, a name for Aotearoa's North Island in a major Māori tradition.
- **Te Waka-a-Māui:** Māui's canoe, a name associated with the South Island in that tradition.
- **Rakiura:** linked to the anchor in a version of the story.
- **Hawaiki:** significant ancestral home/origin realm in differing histories and narratives; not a single invented Disney island on an official map.

Respect living place names and the iwi/hapū who have relationships to them. A fictional location should not be written as a secret unclaimed Māori sacred site without strong narrative reason and consultation.

Sources: https://teara.govt.nz/en/first-peoples-in-maori-tradition ; https://teara.govt.nz/en/document/3802/maui

## Ocean ecology and kaitiakitanga

A sea is not infinite treasure. The kaitiakitanga framework emphasizes responsibilities to the place, resource protection, mauri, mana, tapu and rāhui. A character can be powerful enough to take a catch and still **not have the right** to take it. This aligns particularly well with The Shore I Choose's seasonal fish counts, tide/safety ledgers and the distinction between a spectacular rescue and sustaining a functioning working pier.

Source: https://teara.govt.nz/en/kaitiakitanga-guardianship-and-conservation/page-5

## Tattoos across Pacific cultures

Disney Maui's animated tattoos function as visual trophies, memories and comic conscience. Across Pacific tattoo traditions, tattooing can communicate genealogy, standing, life events, artistry, obligation and social relationships—never merely a generic magic power. **Tā moko** specifically is a Māori artform with distinct techniques and a tradition linked to Niwareka, Mataora and Uetonga. An accurate account does not flatten it into other Polynesian tattooing practices.

In the fanfiction, Mini Maui may move as a **Disney animated-fiction device**; Jasper shouldn't take that as proof that every Māori person's moko moves or has magical consciousness.

## Music, material and craftsmanship

Traditional taonga pūoro instruments (including kōauau, pūtōrino, pūtātara, pūkāea) have differing voices, materials, social functions and traditions. Their making involves craft and expert knowledge; there is a modern history of revitalization. Avoid assigning the same sonic effect to all 'tribal flutes'.

Source: https://teara.govt.nz/en/maori-musical-instruments-taonga-puoro

## Micro-scenes grounded in believable work

- A wet rope stretches slightly; a worker refuses to accept a hero's knot without checking it.
- Children observe weather with a respected elder, but may still make bad predictions.
- The cost of fishing isn't just going out to sea; there is labor in drying, salting, equitable distribution and storage.
- A damaged musical instrument has an owner and craftsperson; no one gets to borrow it without consent.
- A performer asks the audience not to film; Maui backs them up and doesn't turn it into a speech about his own heroism.
- Jasper uses a safe access route to the dock even when Maui can leap there in one step.
