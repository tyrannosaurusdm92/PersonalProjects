# Annotated research sources and provenance

Compiled 5 November 2026. Preference is given to Māori-focused cultural institutions, a tribunal's attributed evidence, museum and government explainers, Disney for its own fiction, and university collections for Hawaiian variants. Links were located in web research on this date. Some Te Ara 1966 historical pages expressly warn they are not updated and need more recent/context-specific corroboration.

## Maui and myth

**M01 — Te Ara, First peoples in Māori tradition**
https://teara.govt.nz/en/first-peoples-in-maori-tradition
For Māui's fish, Te Ika-a-Māui, and links with creation and other ancestor traditions. Good mainstream Māori-specific overview; does not replace tribal narratives.

**M02 — Te Ara, Kaumātua in early traditions**
https://teara.govt.nz/en/kaumatua-maori-elders/page-2
For Māui's grandfather, Murirangawhenua, Mahuika and Hine-nui-te-pō as sources of teaching, power or consequence.

**M03 — Te Ara, Taranga artwork description**
https://teara.govt.nz/en/artwork/27618/taranga
For named mother and tikitiki birth narrative. Museum artwork reproduction has separate permissions; **do not save or reuse the image** without clearance.

**M04 — Te Ara, Māui and grandfather**
https://teara.govt.nz/en/artwork/27619/maui-and-his-grandfather
For a specific version's ancestral raising; do not universalize name and events.

**M05 — Wiremu Grace / Mātauranga Māori, How Māui slowed the sun**
https://eng.mataurangamaori.tki.org.nz/Support-materials/Te-Reo-Maori/Maori-Myths-Legends-and-Contemporary-Stories/How-Maui-slowed-the-sun
A full attributed story featuring collective rope preparation, Tamanuiterā and the jawbone. Link rather than reproduce karakia.

**M06 — Te Ara historic 1966 Maui collection**
https://teara.govt.nz/en/1966/maui-legends-of/page-2
https://teara.govt.nz/en/1966/maui-legends-of/page-5
Useful for recorded variants of parents, birth, fire and death; older editorial framing and the page's own warning require cautious attribution.

**M07 — Waitangi Tribunal, Mana Wāhine evidence on atua wāhine**
https://waitangitribunal.govt.nz/en/inquiries/kaupapa-inquiries/mana-wahine/te-kete-puputu/briefs-of-evidence-2/who-are-our-atua-whaea-and-what-are-their-characteristics-and-stories/key-evidence-about-atua-whaea-2
Attributed statements about Murirangawhenua and Mahuika; perspectives in evidence, not a universal authority for all iwi.

**M08 — Te Ara, Hine-nui-te-pō / tangihanga**
https://teara.govt.nz/en/tangihanga-death-customs/print
For the death narrative and its relationship to Hine-tītama, as presented by the encyclopedia.

**M09 — Te Ara, Hine-nui-te-pō artwork narrative variant**
https://teara.govt.nz/en/photograph/12154/hine-nui-te-po-and-maui
Records variation in the bird involved. Artwork must not be copied without permission.

## Hawaiʻi and comparative material

**H01 — University of Hawaiʻi, Hawaiian Geological Traditions**
https://www.soest.hawaii.edu/GG/FACULTY/ROWLAND/GG104/Readings/Gon_2001a.pdf
Distinguishes some Hawaiian Māui 'joining islands' traditions from the Māori 'fishing up an island' version. Scholarly teaching context; beware using only a short excerpt to claim every Hawaiian tradition agrees.

**H02 — University of Hawaiʻi, Mānaiakalani**
https://www.hawaii.edu/kawaihapai/manaiakalani/
Hawaiian fishhook/star-family information, **not Māori**.

**H03 — University of Hawaiʻi Hawaiian Legends Index: Maui**
https://www.manoa.hawaii.edu/hawaiiancollection/legends/subjectsearch.php?q=Maui+%28Polynesian+Deity%29
Bibliographic index of Hawaiian textual versions, many historical; use to locate named storytellers, not as a substitute for reading them.

**H04 — National Geographic, Maui, tattoos and Polynesia**
https://www.nationalgeographic.com/culture/article/polynesian-tattoo-oceania-culture-moana-maui
Interviews with tattoo practitioners and discussion of cross-Pacific differences. Helpful contextual secondary source; not a single primary source for all Polynesian myth.

## Research boundaries, unresolved questions

1. Obtain specific contemporary Māori-language and iwi/hapū story tellings before expanding sacred genealogies, named guardians or karakia.
2. Obtain specific Tongan, Samoan and other Pacific primary voices before making strong claims about those variations; current comparative file is intentionally conservative.
3. Source individual historical haka compositions or poi repertoires before quoting/transcribing them.
4. Images and tattoos are **not bundled**. Reuse rights and cultural permissions vary independently of educational access to webpages.
5. This is a researched **writer's sourcebook**, not a peer-reviewed ethnography. A Māori cultural consultant should review cultural details before any commercial or public-facing publication.
