# Maui and the Other Voices — Applied Pass 16 Continuity

**Status:** editorial reference created after direct changes to the existing 50-chapter *The Shore I Choose* manuscript, November 6, 2026. This document does not replace `01_DISNEY_CANON.md`, `13_PERSONALITY_REWRITE_CANON.md`, or the original 50 chapters. It is also not an official film script.

## Maui remains the film's great show-off

He wants a crowd. He is quick to laugh, quicker to embellish, protective, jealous sometimes, physically audacious, good at voyaging and able to turn a small inconvenience into a headline. He resents a no, may sulk before making a better choice, can be clever and genuinely brave, and fears the moment someone notices him only for what he can do. He is not transformed into a quiet all-purpose therapist.

The special magic is **Disney film fiction**: his enchanted hook and animal shifts, a small animated tattoo that sometimes mocks him, and cinematic tales of his deeds. He must not claim the distinct ancestral histories from this folder as provable memories of his Disney film life. Do not script real Māori iwi-specific ceremony, genealogy, songs or tattoo traditions on invented authority. Moana remains a capable and important hero in his history; she is not erased by his love for Jasper.

## Three men whose kindness must not be interchangeable

**Maui:** "I could carry the whole thing!" His first solution is often too grand. He respects the answer while making a comic grievance of it, then is vulnerable when he believes he no longer matters. In romantic scenes his flirtation can be enormous, physical and foolish. Let the hook stay magical and his carpentry knowledge remain limited.

**Iroh:** "You have not yet accounted for the people who must do this work." He might sound thoughtful, but his experience includes directing an army, political danger, Ba Sing Se supply routes, Pai Sho and tea. He can assume too much about a paid rota; Mina can correct him. He has grief for Lu Ten that no new romance should turn into a lesson or erase. He uses a cup, a map or a pun more naturally than an impromptu counselor's monologue.

**Greg Universe:** "Uh, I had a plan. The van disagreed." His thinking wanders through chord changes, broken equipment, Beach City, Steven, work shifts, self-mocking humor and the practicalities of showing up. A hesitant joke and a receipt are more his language than a perfectly formed proverb. He may care about Maui and still keep his own song, set list and family history.

**Severus:** More likely to argue about exact terms or demand a documented stop signal than to explain the moral of the scene. His concern can sound angry or sharp. Precision is not a substitute for Jasper's permission.

**Danse:** Reports torque, amperage, ownership and operational constraints. He can care while struggling to surrender command. A local operator may correct an unusable instruction.

**Aang / Katara / Toph:** Aang can evade an argument through a daring design; Katara insists on medicine and the people who use it; Toph feels unstable metal or a bad roadway and says so unkindly. Their voices are not three versions of Jasper.

## Jasper's voice and relationship conditions

The narrator is an adult **I/me/my** character with incomplete memories in the Maui book, agency in every book, imperfect humor, sensory attention and real work outside romance. Treat the repository's `json/william-writing-style.json` as a prose-cadence and revision aid; do not copy the underlying source corpus verbatim.

Jasper explicitly tells romantic partners they are **ethically non-monogamous** and **prefer polyamory**. That does not make them obligated to publish dates, automatically date everyone together, or explain all private partnerships to a village. Partners set and revise their own boundaries; a character may feel jealous or want reassurance without controlling Jasper. Romantic development should include different consequences in each book, not seven identical consent speeches.

Warmth can be sensual while remaining non-explicit: a pause at a doorway, a hand on a rain-soaked sleeve, an awkward kiss interrupted by kitchen duties, or an earned closing of the bedroom door. Use fade-to-black scenes naturally, not as repetitive prizes for doing civic chores.

## Non-regression editorial test

Before editing Maui in any chapter, check:

1. Can Maui still be recognizable without Jasper narrating who he is? A joke, bodily action, brag or impatient reaction should give him away.
2. Does growth change his choice rather than remove his flaws?
3. Does a local expert or owner retain control of the work, memory, art or route?
4. If Mini Maui or the hook is used, does the screen-film device serve this exact scene, rather than replace a character decision?
5. Does Jasper remain able to disagree, decline, leave, desire and change a plan?
6. Do Greg and Iroh sound different when placed beside Maui?
7. Does any new folklore detail falsely conflate Disney Maui with a specific Māori tradition?
8. Does the scene create an observable consequence in the Lanternweald, or merely another polished statement of the lesson?
9. Did the chapter retain its number, ID, title, source and reader override synchronization?
10. Have the actual work hours, transport limits, costs, privacy and world-specific commitments survived the romance?
