# Integrating the research with The Shore I Choose

**Status:** This is a set of editorial opportunities, not a retrospective change to the published novel. All existing chapters and existing reader assets remain untouched.

## Canon guardrails from the existing Book collection

1. **Maui/Jasper is a 50-chapter novel**, The Shore I Choose. Don't create a seventh competing novel or new start scene by mistake.
2. Jasper is the same adult first-person narrator (I/me/my) across six books; in established continuity their relationships are ethically non-monogamous and they prefer polyamory. Maui is a partner, not owner or sole destiny.
3. **Tideglass Cove** is the seafront Lanternweald district. Fen's measured tides, Lio's skiff access and Tamsin's inspections matter. The **sealed historic underground flood passage stays sealed**, even after the **separate temporary surface channel** is tested.
4. The community contains independent characters with jobs, money, schedules and reasons to disagree.
5. The Māori visiting artist **Mereana** and her ensemble have already appeared, particularly around chapters 6 and 25, with a documented distinction between their contemporary work and any claimed universal courtship ritual.
6. Do not overwrite ../docs/MAUI_CULTURAL_NOTES.md; this folder extends its safeguards.
7. Romance remains sensual but non-explicit in the existing novels. Fades to black and consent/aftercare should be character-specific rather than formulaic.

## Six future chapter-level improvements, all optional

### A. The tide is not applause

Scene: Fen refuses a launch time even after Maui jokes he can carry the entire boat. Jasper asks for the actual hazard window. Maui goes quiet, then chooses to help mark the safer route rather than protest. Core mythic echo: slowing the sun did not make every time his to decide. **Original fiction, not a retelling of the sun story.**

### B. Who taught the hero?

Scene: Maui starts an embellished sea story; Jasper asks who made the rope, fed the brothers, taught the knot and gave him the tool. His first response is performative; his second recognizes overlooked women/elders. Sources can shape this moral question without assigning his Disney-film mother the genealogy of a specific iwi account.

### C. Mereana owns the stage

Scene: Mereana has a new piece that draws on her actual professional concerns, but she will not explain her group's private motivations to curiosity-seekers. She negotiates sound, payment, transport, accessibility and the right not to be recorded. Jasper and Maui support rather than center themselves. Her iwi affiliation or exact repertoire should **not** be invented as factual authority without further consultation.

### D. The hook, the body, the memory

Scene: Mini Maui acts comically but Jasper points out that living people's tattoo traditions are not props. A joke lands on Maui's **film-specific magical tattoo**, not on Māori tā moko. He is allowed an awkward, sincere response instead of a lecture.

### E. Guarding without possession

Scene: A visiting community or environmental adviser says a waterway is protected. Maui wants to intervene; Jasper asks whose jurisdiction it is. A local guardian legend might be discussed **by someone entitled to tell it**, rather than becoming a random combat encounter. Or use an entirely original Lanternweald wetland-guardian story with no copied Māori name.

### F. When the hero is not there

Scene: A storm test passes because the workers make it pass, not because Maui knows everything. Maui learns to celebrate people whose names aren't sung, and Jasper notices the difference between how he wants to be seen and how he chooses to act.

## Suggested series of research-informed but original small stories

1. **The Names on the Rope:** a local boatmaker asks the audience to credit the crew who kept it safe; Maui redraws the festival announcement.
2. **After the Rain Gauge:** the tide looks harmless while the measured storm surge says otherwise.
3. **The Song She Did Not Owe Them:** Mereana declines a performance and explains how her chosen work is scheduled.
4. **Another Kind of Strength:** Maui asks an injured worker how to help instead of lifting them without permission.
5. **A Supper Without a Legend:** Jasper's other partners are discussed as real people with agency and calendars rather than rivals for a prize.
6. **The Memory in the Hook:** his film-specific magical hook becomes a starting point for a conversation about memory and responsibility—not a claim about traditional Māori moko.

**All six are proposals only.** Add to actual manuscript only in a separate editorial operation that synchronizes canonical JSON chapters, manifests, reader overlays and chapter counts.

## Consultation gate

For detailed depiction of iwi-specific Māori ceremony, a named taniwha, archival whakapapa, published compositions, or design of traditional moko: stop and get an appropriate Māori consultant or community source. Cite and credit their contribution and respect limitations on reproduction.
