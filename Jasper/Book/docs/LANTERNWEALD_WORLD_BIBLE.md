# The Lanternweald: Shared World Bible

## Original premise
The Lanternweald is a self-contained magical vale threaded between **real, continuing universes**. It is not a replica of a franchise village. At its heart stands the Lanternwell: a spring beneath old stone arches, where small hanging lights glow when someone has found a place they wish to return to.

The vale opens **Threadways**: consenting two-way thresholds connecting familiar places in different worlds. A Threadway cannot force anyone through, steal their memories, heal a wound without consent, undo consequences, or declare a person destined for a relationship. A visitor retains their history and may go home. Days do not line up precisely between homeworlds, but causal events cannot be reversed by crossing a door. No one gets to skip an apology by stepping through a threshold.

## Jasper's place
Jasper is a named adult first-person reader-insert, never a blank puppet or a chosen savior. Their curiosity, humor, boundaries and resourcefulness link the neighborhoods. A forgotten beginning at Tideglass Cove (the Maui story) comes *before* their visits to the five existing worlds in Jasper's personal chronology. Jasper eventually knows and claims their name, without being required to reclaim every expectation attached to it.

## Geography
- **Lanternwell Square:** neutral common ground, community meals, notice board, books, shared repairs, lantern festival.
- **Jasmine Steps / the Jasmine Door:** tea garden and Ba Sing Se; Uncle Iroh's connection to the Jasmine Dragon remains real.
- **Riverstone Terrace / the Riverstone Door:** public works and Cranefish Town; Aang, Katara and Toph retain their own responsibilities and political disagreements.
- **Moonroot Archive / the Moonroot Door:** library, difficult learning and the postwar wizarding world; Severus remains private, exacting and accountable.
- **Brasswatch Yard / the Brasswatch Door:** accessible repair workshop and the Commonwealth; Danse's Brotherhood past is never erased or magically excused.
- **Starwake Pier / the Starwake Door:** music and Beach City; Greg's grief, independence and family remain central.
- **Tideglass Cove / the Tideglass Door:** shore, navigation, Maui and Jasper's first memory fragments.

## How the series fit
Each original 40-chapter story continues in its established homeworld, with the **shared-world bridge scenes** before chapter 1 or after select chapters marking real visits to Lanternweald. These scenes are not alternate endings or choose-your-own-adventure routes; the chapter order stays linear. Every chapter's structured source file identifies its home district and world threshold. Characters may meet across stories at Lanternwell Square, while the individual plots still follow their original emotional stakes.

Crossovers never transform other characters into props. No relationship is erased and no romance is silently made exclusive or nonexclusive: consent, honest conversation, and the specific boundaries established within each story still govern those relationships. Lanternweald does not automatically grant intimacy.

## Everyday life and community conflicts
Residents maintain a shared garden, cook communal meals, mend lamps and roofs, run a lending library, share songs and stories, and disagree about the fair distribution of resources. Conflicts have consequences: a broken bridge needs repair even after a stirring speech. The vale is warm, adventurous and restful, but never a magic shortcut past responsibility.

## Continuity and language
- No lost-memory effect applies to anyone except Jasper at the beginning of the Maui story.
- A Threadway is a door to a *real place*, not an artificial copy of a person or a memory.
- Avoid reusing franchise worldbuilding terminology as the vale's own foundational lore.
- The five original chapter contents are preserved verbatim in Step 3; new scenes live in bridge fields and appear in the live reader.
- No branching decisions or interactive romance routes are introduced.

## First Lantern — origin chronology
The Shore I Choose (10 chapters) is the first personal arc in Jasper's chronology: the tide, memory fragments, Maui, the Lanternwell, and the first five opening Threadways. Its final scene deliberately opens doors toward the established stories. The other stories remain separate but connected complete romances with their own boundaries; no future relationship silently rewrites a past one. The shared vale supports ongoing conversations about change, autonomy, and consent.

## The Seventh Ring — the next shared arc

The first six new continuation chapters (`The Shore I Choose` 11 and the other five series 41) open the **Seventh Ring** mystery. The ring is not a seventh portal, a prophecy, a soulmate test, or a sentient matchmaker. It is a mechanical fail-safe built into the Lanternwell's old balancing system. The six visible rings represent the six active Threadways; the center ring responds when communal cargo obligations cease to match the paths that carry them.

A misleading set of destination labels, a bent brass tooth, a missing ledger page, and a half-remembered song all point to the same problem. **Orren** was given informal authority to optimize deliveries. Orren made changes before asking the districts that depended on the system, believing smoother traffic would justify the shortcut. Orren must remain a person with understandable reasons and real responsibility, not a secret all-powerful villain. The story does not end when the culprit is identified: trust, spoiled supplies, missing records, and damaged public infrastructure still need repair.

The mystery's first evidence appears differently in each book:
- **Maui / Jasper:** the second bell note, salt in the machinery chamber, and the seventh suspended ring; they first invite the district residents to account for their own supplies.
- **Severus / Jasper:** the empty ledger column, misclassified ingredients, the measured scores on the bent tooth, and the principle that a place cannot apologize in a person's stead.
- **Iroh / Jasper:** the delivery of setting tools mistaken for spoons, the delayed medicine jar, and the label written incorrectly before a courier crossed.
- **Aang, Katara, Toph / Jasper:** an amended, non-unanimous meeting motion and a warning claiming the keepers' old system was better than public consent.
- **Danse / Jasper:** the tool marks, the copied tags, Len's sighting of Orren, and the missing page from the records room.
- **Greg / Jasper:** a wrongly delivered handmade flute, the loss and return of his guitar, and a melody whose missing second page connects to an older keeper.

All of these clues are compatible. They are not six separate explanations that will be conveniently overwritten later.

## The original keepers and the Ledger Count

The first keeper circle formed when strangers discovered they could carry seeds, medicine, tools, and books between homes. The founders disagreed about whether a gift had any claim upon the giver. Their compromise became the **Ledger Count**, a public record of *responsibility for things in transit* rather than ownership of people.

A valid transfer answers three questions in ordinary language: **Who offered it? Who agreed to receive it? Who will make things right if it goes missing?** A gift can have no return date, but still has an intended recipient. Borrowed equipment has a return agreement. Emergency aid has priority and may use a designated receiver when its sender cannot stay to complete the paperwork. A traveler does not owe the Lanternwell their private history, relationship status, or medical information in exchange for passage.

The original keepers chose redundant paper records, visible labels, and shared keys so that no spell or single official would be required to explain the system. The seventh ring measures mismatched *transfers*, not a human being's worth. Its motion is a warning about accumulated unresolved records. It can help expose a problem, never decide how people must solve it.

## Threadway travel: precise rules

1. A human being opens and crosses a Threadway voluntarily; neither the bell, a keeper, a lover, nor the reader can force passage or move a person as cargo.
2. Connected universes continue independently. A crossing cannot undo a death, reset a war, replace consent, fabricate a memory, or remove a character's obligations at home.
3. Crossing moves a person forward in their personal sequence. Homeworld clocks can differ, but a missed appointment or broken promise cannot be reversed by searching for a friendlier timestamp.
4. A letter, tool, food parcel, musical instrument, or other inanimate item may be carried across with the traveler's knowledge. Bulk shipments use labels and receiver records. A mislabeled parcel can arrive at the wrong *distribution station*, not telekinetically kidnap its carrier.
5. The well and its lanterns reflect a *chosen return*, not romantic destiny. Light does not prove a relationship is healthy or grant permission to touch.
6. Every threshold may close for safety or repair. Its caretakers must offer an alternate means of contacting affected residents; no keeper is entitled to make isolation a punishment.
7. A community can adopt safer procedures, but nobody can use the well's magic to override a person's informed refusal. There is no romance-compatibility power hidden in the architecture.

## The Vale as a lived-in community

**Lanternwell Square** has a notice board, shared kitchens, a community table, a water channel, and the bell chamber beneath the stone. It is noisy at mealtimes and unglamorous after a storm; somebody always has to clean the grate.

**Jasmine Steps (east)** supports a small tea stall, garden terraces, and shelves of donated ceramics. It remains separate from the real Jasmine Dragon in Ba Sing Se. Iroh can choose which shop needs him on a given day, and his customers do not cease to exist because he visits the vale.

**Riverstone Terrace (southeast)** has practical stonework, shared plans for bridges, gathering benches, and disagreements about accessibility, public works, and consent to use communal labor. The door leads to Cranefish Town, where the adult team's political responsibilities continue.

**Moonroot Archive (northwest)** lends books, maps, translations, and carefully labeled medicine reference material. It maintains private reading tables. Severus does not automatically have the authority to inspect anybody's correspondence.

**Brasswatch Yard (north)** contains an accessible repair bench, tool sign-out, bell-metal maintenance, and fire-safety rules. It does not allow Brotherhood hierarchy or Commonwealth prejudices to govern the workers.

**Starwake Pier (west)** hosts rehearsals, small concerts, weatherproof storage, and boats whose owners are responsible for their moorings. Greg's guitar and the unfinished keeper melody are not mystical keys that resolve every clue.

**Tideglass Cove (southwest)** has a sheltered dock, wild coastal herbs, and the first path where Jasper began to reclaim their name. The brass key with blue thread is a personal object, not a device that can compel others to open doors.

### Work, food, and disagreement

Goods are scarce in different ways across the six worlds. A magical herb, battery, ration pack, canvas sail, or sheet music can be precious for reasons not represented by its price. Each district retains a say in resources that are necessary for its own survival. A person may donate freely; a community must not quietly treat another world's generosity as an unlimited supply.

The Lanternwell council is an *ad hoc* gathering of affected people, not six permanent rulers. Votes can fail. Meetings can reconvene. Unanimity is appropriate for decisions directly changing a household's boundaries; practical public works may require different procedures agreed by their users. The vale does not dissolve all conflict with speeches. Someone must still carry the medicine, rebuild the chair, and return the wrench.

## Social consequences and romance continuity

Jasper is an adult, named first-person protagonist who **practices ethical non-monogamy and prefers polyamory**. They say this plainly to Maui, Severus, Iroh, Aang, Katara, Toph, Danse, and Greg in their own chapters, before assuming romantic exclusivity. The conversations do not constitute automatic agreement. Each partner retains the right to ask questions, set terms, prefer monogamy, negotiate or decline a romance, request privacy, and reconsider with honesty. Love does not erase jealousy or make different people's needs identical.

Every romance should contain its own forms of flirtation, miscommunication, humor, physical tenderness, and repair. Keep the first major intimate turning point emotionally detailed but non-graphic; later recurring private nights can end naturally in a fade to black. Intimacy must never be payment for hospitality, healing, a favor, or safe passage. Public affection requires its participants' consent, and no person becomes Jasper's partner merely because they appear in a crossover scene.

Aang, Katara, and Toph are explicitly **adults** during their story's romantic plot. Each has independent preferences and boundaries. Greg remains Steven's father with a life outside the romance; Iroh retains Lu Ten's memory and his responsibilities to Zuko; Severus must own the harm he caused; Danse must reckon with the Brotherhood; Maui's myths and performances must not be flattened into a false single Polynesian tradition.

### Shared story chronology

Jasper's **first** story begins at Tideglass Cove, where their name and agency return. The other five book arcs follow the opening of the doors in Jasper's personal chronology but may overlap in Lanternweald visits; each world's home events remain intact. The first 40 chapters of the five established romances form their own complete emotional arcs. Chapters 41–50 are new connected **continuation arcs**, not replacement endings. For Maui, chapters 11–50 build a second, longer story around memory, consent, and the community Jasper chose, without reversing the conclusion of chapter 10.

**Continuity gate:** Before writing a new chapter, verify the latest keeper clues, current inventories, who has heard which information, what Jasper has promised each person, the homeworld stakes, and what remains unresolved. A character should not know an off-world scene merely because it exists in another book. A crossover must change a decision or illuminate a relationship, not only list familiar names.
