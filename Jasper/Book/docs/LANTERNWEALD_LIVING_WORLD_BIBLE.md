# The Lanternweald — Living World Bible, Expansion Edition

> **Editorial status:** This expands `LANTERNWEALD_WORLD_BIBLE.md`; it does not replace or retcon it. Existing six stories, homeworld consequences, and their characters remain canon. The Lanternweald is **not** a copy of Dreamlight Valley. Its appeal is a similarly lived-in web of districts, residents, traditions, errands, discoveries and relationships, with an original history and rules.

## 1. The promise of the place

The Lanternweald is a real, bounded vale between continuing worlds, rather than a collection of staged fantasies. People have homes and lives on both sides of its Threadways. A traveler can return home, decline an invitation, change their mind, or choose not to be touched. The vale does not heal grief, replace an apology, rewrite a war, erase a partner, resurrect a loved one, or reward Jasper with another person's affection. Its miracle is much smaller: it makes meeting possible, then lets people decide what to do with that chance.

The heart is **Lanternwell Square**, a spring under three weathered stone arches. Its hanging lights brighten when someone chooses to return, not when someone is conquered, paired, or declared worthy. Jasper is not a prophesied ruler, mechanic, monarch or magical cure. Their gift is remembering what others overlook: who needs a ramp, whose name has been written incorrectly, why a wall cracked even though the ribbon-cutting went beautifully, and who stopped showing up after a disagreement.

### The five governing rules

1. **Invitation, never summons.** A Threadway opens to travelers who consent; it never drags people into another world. Someone can shut their side of a door.
2. **Consequences remain.** A wound needs treatment. A damaged pump still needs parts. A mistake at a homeworld council cannot be fixed by passing through the vale.
3. **No borrowed rights.** Arrival gives no right to local property, position, secrets, labor, romantic access, or magic. A visitor is a neighbor before they are anything else.
4. **Memory belongs to its owner.** The bell can help Jasper recognize attached memories; it cannot edit someone else's mind. Jasper's initial amnesia is specific to Jasper's arrival. It is not an epidemic and not a device for every other character.
5. **Crossings leave a trace.** Each freely used Threadway warms the Lanternwell and hangs a new pinprick of light in the arches. Forced or reckless crossings stress the stone, dim neighboring lamps and can strand people on the *side they chose to enter*, until the structure is repaired. Doors must never be used to imprison.

## 2. The seven neighborhoods

### Lanternwell Square — the shared table
A cobbled center with the well, bakery, lending shelf, community notice board, festival hooks and four permanently mismatched tables. Neri maintains the arrival book but does **not** keep a list of romances; everyone has the right to privacy. A publicly posted errand may be claimed or declined without a penalty. Neighbors take turns sweeping, baking, checking railings and organizing accessibility improvements. Children are allowed to be loud. The loudest regular adult is generally Maui.

**Recurring scenes:** morning bread, public votes with actual dissent, lantern-light repairs, crowded tea breaks, second chances after badly worded conversations. **Long mystery:** the oldest stone arch bears six scratches although Neri insists only five doors were expected after Tideglass.

### Tideglass Cove — home chosen, not inherited
A salt-bright coast with tidal stairs, a sheltered launch, an abandoned signal post, weather-mottled sailcloth, sea-glass beds and the Tideglass Door. Maui and Jasper repaired the first landing together. It now requires a posted tide chart because several residents are tired of choosing between wet boots and drowning. Small boats belong to their owners; the vale does not conjure free transportation. Mereana and other visitors from Aotearoa are visiting artists with their own schedules and authority; Māori traditions are not generic magic mechanics or romantic accessories.

**Recurring scenes:** knots, boat maintenance, short voyages, difficult memories, weather, honest affection after a joke fails. **Long mystery:** bells heard under low tide answer the well's bell a heartbeat late.

### Jasmine Steps — hospitality with boundaries
Terraced walkways smell of tea, rain on brick and flowering vines. The **Jasmine Door** opens toward real Ba Sing Se, not a museum copy. Iroh runs occasional tastings in the vale and returns to the Jasmine Dragon's real obligations; others must learn to make their own tea. A little tile workshop makes cups with repaired seams visible on purpose. Hospitality here means offering, *not* insisting.

**Recurring scenes:** Pai Sho matches with differing stakes, tea-tin labels, respectful disagreements about grief, quiet nighttime confessions and public celebrations where Iroh is a man, never merely a sage. **Long mystery:** one planting bed thrives only when its steward stops trying to force it into bloom.

### Riverstone Terrace — the argument that builds
A patchwork neighborhood with masonry tests, shared wells, bridges and the **Riverstone Door** to Cranefish Town. Its water committee has no automatic right to redirect resources from the real town. Aang carries political and cultural responsibilities; Katara insists medical access is not a festival afterthought; Toph can tell which foundations are dishonest but is not a magical lie detector for every conversation. Jasper audits deliveries and makes unpopular but necessary lists.

**Recurring scenes:** bridge permits, access ramps, workers' wages, family dinner, romantic disagreements handled by *each* individual, air-kite days and hard-won laughter. **Long mystery:** a repaired bridge develops one extra step during storms, only when visitors are too hurried to notice their neighbors.

### Moonroot Archive — knowledge is not entitlement
A public library and protected workroom with deep-green shutters, a herb garden, book repair benches and the **Moonroot Door** to the wizarding world. Severus may consult on dangerous materials but is not forced into public emotional labor; his history remains subject to scrutiny. Readers must sign a record before handling objects from other worlds. A locked shelf means *ask*, not *steal*. The archive hosts skill-sharing evenings with practical demonstrations rather than lectures disguised as forgiveness.

**Recurring scenes:** catalog disputes, patient research, responsible antidotes, the politics of access and the awkward tenderness of competence. **Long mystery:** a ledger contains a blank line for a librarian whose name no one will write.

### Brasswatch Yard — making, measuring, mending
A scaffolded repair district with accessible benches, a covered forge, spare-parts shelves, rainwater tanks and the **Brasswatch Door** to the Commonwealth. Danse uses a ledger, not a chain of command. His Brotherhood history and fear of losing structure cannot vanish at the doorway. Toph is an occasional structural critic; Greg helps when a job needs more enthusiasm than skill; Jasper asks how repairs will work for whoever actually uses them.

**Recurring scenes:** leaky roofs, water filtration, ruined power cells, arguments over whether a repair is safe, and intimacy built through asking before reaching. **Long mystery:** salvage from different worlds sometimes reacts to the well, but neither radiation nor magic may be casually converted into a cure.

### Starwake Pier — songs with room for silence
A broad timber pier with a small listening stage, folding chairs, instruments, strings of lanterns and the **Starwake Door** to Beach City. Music belongs to its writers; the vale does not turn Greg into the designated free entertainer. Steven's family and Greg's previous grief remain part of his life, not obstacles swept aside for Jasper's romance. Some evenings the stage hosts music. Some evenings it hosts people sitting together and saying very little.

**Recurring scenes:** imperfect rehearsals, weather damage, collaboration with Iroh's tea service, children requesting impossible songs and private moments where a joke stops hiding a fear. **Long mystery:** on nights when a door has been strained, one note sounds from the empty stage.

## 3. People who live here

**Neri** is the old keeper of the arrival book: they remember the last closure but refuse to call people "chosen." They misjudge the amount of help they need. Their growth is learning to share records and labor without sharing visitors' private histories. Their walking stick taps twice before they tell a difficult truth.

**Mara** supplies bread from the square bakery and occasionally visits from the Beach City side. She has no patience for romantic speeches that leave dishes in the sink. Her complaint that the common kitchen lacks a decent flour shelf becomes a campaign with budget, votes and a carpentry project.

**Tamsin Vale**, the local lanternwright, can mend a torn lantern but not the relationship it once symbolized. She speaks in specifications until someone asks about her missing apprenticeship partner. Her arc is about guild accountability, not a surprise romantic claim on Jasper.

**Fen Arlow**, the canal steward, keeps tide and runoff marks on every gatepost. He loses an election after covering a safety problem for fear of panic, then has to make repair—not an apology alone—his return to trust.

**Pip**, a young courier, discovers that serving every household makes them everyone's messenger and no one's confidant. Their story introduces privacy: a note sealed for one person is never fair game for the notice board.

These are original residents. Cross-fandom guests retain their own agency and may refuse any errand, celebration or relationship proposal.

## 4. Daily systems that actually affect the stories

**Arrival book:** records name or chosen alias, arrival date, safety needs, intended return door if volunteered, and whether the person consents to public announcement. No relationship registry.

**Repair ledger:** records work, materials, safety checks, who benefits, and who maintains the result next season. A fix without a maintainer is an unfinished fix. Jasper, Danse and Toph can disagree about measurements without one becoming a prop for another.

**Community pantry:** supported by voluntary contributions and clear sharing rules. Food is neither magical infinite supply nor a substitute for the economic systems of the homeworlds. Iroh cooks because he wants to; he can also be tired.

**Garden plots:** mountain mint, jasmine, rosemary, hardy greens, sea kale and ordinary fruit depending on microclimate. Magic affects light at the well, not instant harvests. A hot summer can still ruin seedlings. A fungus problem needs an actual response.

**Lending library:** books are returned, sometimes late; damaged books require repair. Crossworld literature is handled with care because the truth about another world can be sensitive or dangerous. The archive may decline a request.

**Accessibility charter:** crossings and public projects should be usable by more than one kind of body. If a railing, ramp, seat or doorway fails somebody, the committee fixes its own design instead of treating that person as the problem. This is a shared civic value, not a special favor to Jasper.

**Open-sky nights:** one voluntary evening per month when no one is assigned a task unless something is genuinely unsafe. Aang can teach a peaceful kite pattern; Greg can play; Toph can complain; everyone can simply attend.

## 5. Calendar and ritual

A Lanternweald year follows local seasons rather than matching a single homeworld clock. Threshold crossings have to be coordinated by consent and local appointments; time does not reverse or produce a fresh younger copy of anyone.

- **First Thaw / Mending Day:** roof surveys and patching, acknowledgments of unfinished promises.
- **High Bloom / Six Tables:** neighbors bring a meal or simply themselves. Seating is not assigned by relationship status.
- **Longlight / The Listening Fair:** skilled people share crafts, stories or silence; visitors are credited for knowledge they choose to share.
- **Goldfall / The Count of Hands:** publicly audited inventory, winter plans and a chance to challenge committee decisions.
- **Frostturn / The Lantern Vigil:** lights hung for those absent, estranged, dead or elsewhere. It is not a forced reconciliation ceremony.
- **Tidewake / the Shore Feast:** marks the first safe landing, honors work completed with permission, and never treats a visitor's culture as spectacle.

The community's traditions can change through discussion. A festival is not proof that a damaged relationship is repaired.

## 6. The deep history and the season-long plot

**Before the doors:** the vale had the spring, the seven neighborhoods as rough paths, and the ability to keep lamps burning longer than oil should allow. Small families cared for the arches without claiming to own them.

**The Closed Bells:** an earlier keeper attempted to prevent departures after a dangerous winter. The well dimmed and crossings became unreliable. No villain cursed everyone's memories; the harm was the belief that care could justify control. Neri was young enough to watch the repair and old enough to remember the shame. The blank ledger line belongs to the person who argued for reopening the gates. Their name is absent because the keeper refused to write it, not because magic devoured it.

**The Tideglass Arrival:** Jasper arrived without a reliable history, carrying a key and blue thread. Maui helped them learn that a memory is information, not an order. The repaired landing and the first Lantern festival opened the other Threadways.

**Season One — The First Six Lights:** the five established romances occur in their respective worlds, each with real consequences; periodic voluntary visits make acquaintances of people who might otherwise never meet. Crossings begin to show small stress cracks when too much is demanded of one shared structure.

**Season Two — The Weight of Welcome:** the vale becomes popular. A flood damages a Riverstone support; the pantry runs short; someone suggests restricting exits "for safety." It sounds reasonable because everyone is afraid of repeating the Closed Bells. Jasper, Neri, Toph, Danse, Katara, Iroh, Severus, Greg, Maui and local stewards disagree—sometimes sharply—about what safety means. No single rescuer solves it. The answer requires a consent-based exit protocol, maintenance money, more trained hands, and the humility to allow a person to go.

**Season Three — The Seventh Scratch:** the scratch on the arch is not a hidden new franchise portal or destined lover. It marks a **service path** leading beneath the well, formerly used to move water through the vale without interfering with personal thresholds. That route was closed when the keeper controlled exits. Restoring it restores storm drainage; it does not force doors or unlock anyone's memories. Neri must publicly say what happened. A neighbor who still refuses forgiveness is allowed to refuse it.

**Resolution — A place to return to:** the well's light endures because the community maintains it, not because every story ends in an identical vow. Jasper and their partners may visit, stay, leave, sleep, argue, exchange gifts, love and protect their private time. The vale is a home with work to do, not a reward screen.

## 7. Crossover scene bank: social continuity, not cameos

- Iroh and Greg disagree gently about whether a grief song should be played at a public dinner; both learn to ask *whose grief* and *whose choice*.
- Danse and Toph inspect a railing: Danse brings measuring tools; Toph senses instability through stone; neither can replace the other's method. Jasper spots the missing accessibility clearance.
- Severus and Katara argue over the ethics of using scarce ingredients on a universal remedy instead of treating known individual patients first; the solution is an actual allocation plan.
- Aang and Maui compete to make a festival attractive, then find out the neighborhood requested quieter hours and more seating. The lesson is in changed behavior, not a lecture.
- Toph teaches Greg how to notice rhythm through vibration in a beam; he teaches her the pleasure of a song whose tempo refuses to settle. Neither is reduced to a joke.
- Neri asks Jasper to preserve a blank page in the archive for stories not yet given permission to be told.

## 8. Romance and ethics across all six books

Jasper's identity and preference are **ethically non-monogamous, polyamory preferred**. This is a relationship value, not a claim on anyone else's future. Each partner hears it openly, **before romantic exclusivity could be assumed**, and may say yes, no, or "I need time." If a partner agrees, the story still names their own boundaries: what information is private, how schedules work, when jealousy needs care, how sexual health conversations will be handled when relevant, and what kind of emotional responsibility each person can actually offer. A prior "yes" is not permanent permission.

Individual novels keep distinct romantic temperature and voice:
- **Maui:** loud teasing, vulnerable pauses, hands resting near each other before touch, his heroic flourish made small by real attention.
- **Iroh:** shared chores, watchful kindness, tea and unforced invitations, grief with room for laughter.
- **Aang / Katara / Toph:** three separate relationships plus a group practice, direct negotiation, uneven time and care without one voice swallowing the others.
- **Danse:** protective attention checked against military conditioning, deliberate permission, competence that learns how not to command.
- **Severus:** precision and sparse approval, argument carrying feeling, privacy and wit that survive tenderness.
- **Greg:** rambling humor, guitar strings and ordinary work, grief acknowledged without treating Jasper as a replacement.

The first private moment in each narrative may be especially *sensual* (breath, warmth, fabric, hesitation, touch, mutual invitation), but not explicit. Later intimate nights should vary in context and be handled by **natural fade-to-black**, followed by consequence: breakfast, an awkward joke, a repaired disagreement, fatigue, joy, a missed appointment or a remembered promise. **No interchangeable stock bedroom paragraph.**

## 9. Continuity guardrails for authors

1. Write **Jasper in first person** with I/me/my/mine; external characters generally use they/them.
2. The person who walked through all six Threadways is one adult Jasper, not six silent exclusive copies. The narratives are focused arcs within a continuing life; not all events happen on the same day.
3. Do not add branching prompts, CYOA routes, interactive choice endings or explicit scenes.
4. Keep the current 210 existing chapters intact except for carefully placed, canon-compatible relationship-disclosure inserts; do not erase established grief, first kisses or endings.
5. Do not claim a specific Māori haka, poi, waiata, or courtship ritual without proper context and cultural consultation. Existing cultural notes govern the Maui story.
6. Keep each crossing voluntary, keep all visitors' agency, and give every crossover a practical purpose beyond name-checking.
7. A chapter is complete only when it has distinct action, dialogue, changed understanding and a consequence. An outline, bridge note or stub is **not** a finished chapter.
