# The Lanternweald: Six Connected 50-Chapter Novels — Editorial Roadmap

**This document distinguishes published prose from unwritten plans.** Existing published chapters remain in the repository. A roadmap entry is not a finished chapter, and this file must not be counted by the reader as prose.

## Shared craft requirements

Every new chapter should aim for **1,200–1,800 meaningful words** (Maui's existing ten chapters need fuller redevelopment); a shorter chapter is acceptable only if its dramatic arc is genuinely complete. Keep Jasper in first person, character-specific dialogue, physical scene grounding, changing stakes, and consequential endings. Embed explicit, informed ENM/polyamory conversations before romantic commitment in all six series; later chapters revisit agreements when things change. Sensuality remains non-graphic, with varied fade-to-black transitions and ordinary morning-after consequences. Each story retains a separate emotional plot while crossovers gradually build a common civic mystery. Do not insert 90 hollow placeholders just to reach 50.

## Chronology

1. **The Shore I Choose 01–10**: Jasper arrives, names themself and helps establish the Lanternweald; the other Threadways open.
2. **The five other stories 01–40**: individual relationship arcs in their *real* homeworlds; voluntary short bridge scenes show connections.
3. **The five books 41–50 and Maui 11–50**: linked season-two threads that may overlap. Maui 11–20 establishes the civic conflict; 21–30 introduces visitors, 31–40 investigates the Closed Bells, and 41–50 resolves the drainage/exit crisis. The order is not identical to an implied single calendar; no chapter rewinds cause and effect.

## The Shore I Choose — Maui

**Existing prose:** Chapters 1–10. **Planned expansion:** Chapters 11–50 (40 distinct beats). Each planned chapter has a homeworld consequence and a relationship consequence; later crossover appearances must respect each visitor's consent and schedule.

**11. The Tide Takes Inventory** — Jasper and Maui audit storm damage at the first landing and discover that the welcome sign promises a safe crossing that the dock cannot yet provide; fixing a fact becomes the first test of leadership.

**12. A Place for the Departing** — A visiting traveler asks how to leave without giving a reason. Maui wants to offer a heroic escort; Jasper drafts a private departure protocol and they argue about protection versus control.

**13. Ink on the Water** — A current carries a scrap of Neri's oldest ledger ashore. Jasper recognizes the key notch in its drawing, then chooses not to turn a personal memory into the entire village's mystery.

**14. The Quiet Work of Rope** — Maui's hand is hurt when a mooring snaps; Jasper learns a more reliable knot from Fen, and Maui must accept help that does not make him indebted.

**15. No One Is a Prize** — A feast includes a boastful visitor who calls Jasper Maui's reward. Jasper challenges the language publicly; Maui must decide whether his laughter helped the insult.

**16. The Sea Glass Orchard** — Tamsin proposes a sheltered garden of salt-resistant plants. Jasper wants everyone invited; the garden steward explains why not every patch of land can hold every crop.

**17. A Lantern for the Absent** — At Frostturn, Jasper hangs a lamp without naming everyone they once loved. Maui asks how polyamory and their growing affection can coexist with grief and privacy.

**18. One Door Closed by Choice** — Jasper shuts the door to their old room for one night, then tells Maui that postponement is not the same as rejection; they spend an intimate evening together, privately.

**19. The Apprenticeship of Asking** — Maui volunteers to mentor a new boatbuilder and learns that a grand rescue can ruin someone else's chance to learn; Jasper's own impatience is exposed in the same project.

**20. The Second Bell** — A bell sounds below low tide just after the Lanternwell answers. Neri confesses an old maintenance route exists but refuses to say why it was sealed.

**21. The Guest Who Stayed** — Greg arrives with two broken chairs and a song he cannot finish. Maui is competitive until Jasper catches them both ignoring the person who actually asked for help.

**22. Tea, Salt and Smoke** — Iroh visits Tideglass, using a tea steeping timer to teach patience without lecturing. Maui tells him about wanting to be chosen; Iroh admits choosing can frighten him too.

**23. The Wrong Kind of Hero** — Aang invites Maui to build festival decorations; their cheerful competition strains the arch until Katara ends it. Jasper refuses to reward spectacle with an apology nobody acts on.

**24. A Place to Put Jealousy** — A new invitation to Jasper sparks a difficult conversation; Maui names his fear without claiming veto power and Jasper states what reassurance can and cannot promise.

**25. Mereana Returns** — Mereana's visiting group returns on its own terms. She corrects a visitor's mistaken claim that the group's performance is a universal courtship rite; Jasper supports her without taking over.

**26. The Price of Welcome** — The pantry's stores run low after a flood. Maui proposes fishing all night; Katara asks who will salt, store and distribute the catch, and Jasper has to ask neighbors instead of deciding for them.

**27. The Visitor's Right to Leave** — A frightened newcomer is accused of disloyalty for returning home. Jasper helps them leave safely; Maui's public support costs him the adoration of an admirer.

**28. The Spare Key** — Neri asks Jasper to hold an archive key. Jasper refuses to accept authority without accountability and insists the council see a record of what it opens.

**29. A Festival Without a Hero** — Children build the Tidewake stage with adults offering guidance. Maui tries not to perform; a quiet joke shared with Jasper becomes more meaningful than applause.

**30. The Edge of the Storm** — The repaired shore gate fails an inspection. Danse and Toph disagree about repair tolerances; Jasper learns that listening to specialists does not exempt them from a final choice.

**31. Six Different Roads** — Jasper returns from visits with Iroh, Aang, Katara, Toph, Severus, Danse and Greg carrying joy and difficult responsibilities; Maui asks what he should know, not what he may own.

**32. The Shape of an Agreement** — A living relationship agreement is revised after a missed visit. No one is put on trial; Jasper and Maui discuss scheduling, privacy, honesty and the right to say no.

**33. The Unfinished Song** — Greg asks Maui to help with a chorus and receives a speech. He asks again, this time for quiet company. Maui learns that presence is sometimes the correct kind of contribution.

**34. A Nail from the Commonwealth** — Danse's salvaged fastener corrodes in sea air. Brasswatch and Tideglass must test materials together; the failed part has an ordinary explanation with extraordinary consequences.

**35. The Archive's Empty Line** — Severus finds an erased archival name and refuses to fill it with a guess. Jasper meets Neri's shame with questions that require answers, not comforting platitudes.

**36. Permission for Privacy** — A rumor about Jasper's partners reaches Lanternwell Square. Jasper publicly defends everyone's right not to disclose private relationship details to curious neighbors.

**37. The Tide Between Us** — Jasper and Maui disagree about an unannounced visitor. The argument follows them home; the repair begins with each naming a concrete mistake and ends behind a gently closed door.

**38. A Bridge with Too Many Owners** — Aang, Katara, Toph, Jasper and the local committee confront a bridge project funded by one neighborhood and used by two. The real conflict is ongoing maintenance.

**39. The Keeper's Refusal** — Neri finally says they watched an earlier keeper prevent exits under the name of protection. Jasper refuses the neat fiction that a well-intentioned wrong did no harm.

**40. A Day of Ordinary Things** — Maui does laundry badly, Jasper makes two wrong measurements, and everyone eats a late dinner. Small mistakes become evidence that a home can survive imperfection.

**41. Under the Seventh Scratch** — The old drainage channel appears under the arches; its door opens onto maintenance stone, not a new lover or another world. Jasper and Maui descend only after safety checks.

**42. Where the Water Cannot Go** — Fen explains how the sealed service path worsened flooding for decades. Neri provides records; the neighborhood chooses a temporary closure despite the disappointment.

**43. The Right to Disagree** — A public meeting splits over opening the route. Jasper loses a vote and accepts it; Maui stops treating dissent as an insult to their work.

**44. Three Safe Ways Home** — A working group designs emergency exits with clear announcements, private refusal and safe waiting spaces. Each visiting character contributes without becoming an appointed savior.

**45. The Last Big Performance** — Maui is offered a stage during the repair celebration and chooses a smaller role, helping Pip set lantern hooks. Jasper sees him without needing the crowd to see him.

**46. What I Remember Now** — An old memory returns: Jasper used to avoid saying what they wanted. They write down an honest wish for each connection without asking anyone else to fulfill it.

**47. Night at the Reopened Channel** — Rain tests the drainage path. In the quiet hours, Maui and Jasper celebrate surviving a difficult year with tenderness and a private fade-to-black.

**48. The Lantern Ledger** — Jasper and Neri rebuild the old record, retaining a blank line where permission to name someone has not been given. Accountability requires legibility, not forced revelation.

**49. The Boats That Return** — A visitor returns by choice months after leaving. Maui watches the reunion without claiming credit; Jasper learns the beauty of an invitation that can be refused.

**50. The Shore I Keep Choosing** — The final celebration does not end the other romances or declare a single forever. Jasper and Maui repair a small loose board, speak their current promises, and walk toward a door that remains freely open.

## After Always — Severus Snape

**Existing prose:** Chapters 1–40. **Planned expansion:** Chapters 41–50 (10 distinct beats). Each planned chapter has a homeworld consequence and a relationship consequence; later crossover appearances must respect each visitor's consent and schedule.

**41. The Margins We Leave Open** — A Moonroot book returns with unauthorized annotations that could lead a student to a dangerous mixture. Severus wants it locked; Jasper proposes a repairable catalog and a teaching plan.

**44. Three Unsigned Letters** — Old letters from the war arrive at the archive. Severus chooses which to answer himself; Jasper refuses to make his past available to the committee.

**45. The Terms of Us** — After an awkward scheduling conflict, Jasper and Severus revisit ENM, discretion, time and what support is actually requested. Sparse tenderness follows a difficult conversation.

**46. A Book That Would Not Burn** — The archival ledger holds a blank line for the Closed Bells. Severus insists the record cannot be restored by inventing testimony, and helps Neri find the surviving evidence.

**47. A Teacher, Not a Verdict** — A student repeats the heroic version of Severus's history. He corrects it without inviting punishment; Jasper admires his decision to tell an uncomfortable truth.

**48. The Lantern Vigil** — At Frostturn, Greg plays a soft instrumental piece while Severus stands apart. Jasper joins him only after asking and lets his silence remain his.

**49. A Night Without Explanations** — Severus and Jasper have a rare unhurried evening of books, unglamorous dinner and delicate touch. The door closes; the morning asks them to keep an appointment.

**50. After the Door Opens** — Severus volunteers a new cataloging hour on his own terms. Jasper makes tea badly, they argue about punctuation, and the archive light remains on for whoever comes back.

## Jasmine After Rain — Iroh

**Existing prose:** Chapters 1–40. **Planned expansion:** Chapters 41–50 (10 distinct beats). Each planned chapter has a homeworld consequence and a relationship consequence; later crossover appearances must respect each visitor's consent and schedule.

**41. The Cost of a Free Cup** — The Jasmine Steps pantry cannot keep giving away tea without accounting for water and leaves. Iroh and Jasper redesign hospitality so no one is shamed for needing help.

**46. A Game Neither of Us Wins** — Iroh loses a Pai Sho game because he was distracted by fear of being left. Jasper names the fear without offering exclusivity as a cure; they revisit their agreements.

**47. The Empty Chair Is Not a Threat** — A regular customer stops attending. Jasper and Iroh help without presuming a reason; grief enters the room as a person rather than a lesson.

**48. The Dragon and the Demigod** — Maui proposes a dramatic lantern lighting that violates the fire plan. Iroh helps him make something quieter that still feels joyful.

**49. The Bell at Daybreak** — Jasmine Steps' terrace drains begin flowing backward during a storm. Iroh takes the kitchen crew seriously while Jasper helps track the blocked service route.

**50. Tea for Whoever Returns** — After the repairs, Iroh and Jasper leave one table unreserved. Their private happiness and public duties coexist without being merged, and a cold morning ends with two warm cups.

## Editorial checks before publishing each new chapter

- Confirm prior chapter's ending, date, injuries, living arrangements, relationship agreements and still-open problems.
- Include a scene that changes something, not just a recap of values or a long conversation without action.
- Let secondary characters disagree and retain their own concerns, jobs and homes.
- Include emotional friction, memorable physical details, awkward flirtation, shared work and genuine humor in the protagonist's established voice.
- Make ENM informed and revisable; no character becomes automatically polyamorous because Jasper prefers it.
- Never describe an outline as prose or inflate the reader's chapter counter until the chapter exists in complete JSON and the offline reader bundle.
- Read full chapters aloud for repeated phrases, artificial dialogue, missing motivations and abrupt endings.
