## Seven district roles and everyday institutions

### Lanternwell Square — arrivals, food and notices

Morning: Mara records bread stock, Neri handles arrival questions without compelling disclosure of a person's private destination, Pip checks public maps and obstructions. Afternoon: vendors pay for supplies, repair crews post safe-route status, travelers may leave or stay without surrendering a name. Evening: festival and shelter lights have actual loading/turning requirements; a tall lantern stand cannot block Edda's route merely because it looks attractive.

Stories to reuse: a map updated because a resident reports a blocked turn; someone requesting public help without a public personal biography; a bakery running out of a particular item but offering alternatives. Do not reward visitors for handing over private names.

### Tideglass Cove — tidework and sustainable fishing

Fen reads tides and silt rather than pretending weather is predictable. Lio's apprentice work requires safe rigging, real time and supervision. Maui can lift heavy beams and brace storm structures, but only within a plan that considers evacuation and erosion. Mereana retains authority over loaned cultural artwork. The damaged traveler arch from Maui's early story and the historic Closed Bells underground barrier are separate structures. The surface channel's silt line can worsen after storms even when its larger design passes tests.

Stories to reuse: an oyster bed opening late because of salinity; a mooring closed to protect a young sailor; a repair quote whose cost is shared only after the beneficiaries agree.

### Moonroot Archive — public evidence and private testimony

The old ledger remains preserved **as evidence of its faults**, not as an authority over travelers. Orin's new ledger supports dated public corrections and private contributor-led revisions. Severus checks citations and can recognize a student finding an error without taking over the demonstration. Neri handles the difficult fact that a missing signature is still missing. Avel can ask for accountability but cannot fill gaps with a convenient accusation. A ferry-time error may change practical public schedules while leaving historical identity unresolved.

Stories to reuse: a resident brings a repaired receipt and asks to be anonymous; an old tide chart conflicts with a new report; an archive apprentice explains the difference between a summary and testimony with consent.

### Jasmine Steps — teahouse and rainwater infrastructure

Lanternweald's Jasmine Steps has local gardens, paid staff, supplier invoices and its own stormwater repairs. Iroh may visit voluntarily from Ba Sing Se, but no single kettle or business shift exists simultaneously in both worlds. Local winter food stocks and seasonal crop losses can affect the vale without magically changing Mina's records in the homeworld shop.

Stories to reuse: safe washing of herbs, a delivery delayed by a rain-damaged road, customers requesting lower-cost tea, a worker asking to be paid for extra preparation.

### Riverstone Terrace — water and household consent

Katara can identify medical water requirements; Aang may carry heavy containers temporarily while others learn the route; Toph tests ground; Edda evaluates what it means for a pathway to be genuinely accessible. Lio's kite scene reflects those same principles at a different festival: the learner stays the author of their work. Jasper, adult Aang, Katara and Toph keep separate choices about time, intimacy, household tasks and disclosure; no group vote controls Jasper's romances.

Stories to reuse: a dry-run water distribution before the next storm; a light but sturdy kite frame sold by a local craftworker; a household calendar negotiation where no one has to win.

### Brasswatch Yard — repairs, apprenticeship and independent complaint

Danse, Renn, Aster, Tamsin and Ash share a public workshop with different skills and rights to refuse unsafe work. A former military handbook cannot assume a civilian owes a commanding officer an identity report. Ash can use a private room, make a complaint without Danse's permission and decline to participate in a training exercise. Renn can revise procedures and still ask Danse to demonstrate a safe fitting. Electrical maintenance, pay and load limits remain real.

Stories to reuse: a new trainee notices a safety problem in an old template; two workers disagree about who owns a repaired tool; an independent steward reviews a complaint without turning the complainant into a public character.

### Starwake Pier — music, stage safety and volunteers

Greg's performances have chosen sets, proper stage access and staff who can release him when his shift is over. Nola schedules artists and may refuse an unscheduled extra performance; Danse's workshop can repair musical equipment as paid work. The pier's low vibrating note is evidence for a physical investigation, not proof of a supernatural voice. Travel to Beach City requires a voluntary Threadway crossing; Beach City clocks and local weather stay distinct.

Stories to reuse: a short concert delayed by pier maintenance, a volunteer identifying unsafe lantern placement, a recording held back until a guest artist agrees to public release.

## Seasons, schedules and place texture

**Frostturn / winter:** cold, shorter shop hours, wet paved routes, blanket maintenance and a voluntary remembrance walk. A festival cannot overrule a storm closure.

**New spring growth:** runoff and silt inspections, garden cuttings needing control, herb availability and repair materials drying. Reopening a bridge requires measured use, not a ceremonial ribbon.

**Longlight / warmer winds:** kite fabric, hill wind markers, seasonal fishing limits and early evening community meals. Strong gusts can force an early kite-launch closure.

**Tidewake / late-summer to autumn storms:** local children's performances, wet-landings, reserved shelter space, instrument cases kept dry and warning lights checked before festivals.

An inhabited neighborhood should never feel as though every shopkeeper or engineer waits motionless for Jasper to arrive. People can decline a conversation, be on a paid shift, miss a meeting, need a replacement part, return home or disagree honestly.

## Character-specific intimacy and ethical non-monogamy

Jasper is **adult and first-person** throughout. They explicitly tell their partners they are **ethically non-monogamous and prefer polyamory**. Consent is repeatedly negotiated through practice, not sprung as a revelation to create cheap drama. Each partner retains their own preferences and history. Maui's confident humor hides insecurity about being loved without feats. Severus's self-control is sometimes defensiveness, and his private grief deserves privacy. Iroh's warm wisdom is not an unlimited care machine. Aang, Katara and Toph retain independent relationships and viewpoints. Danse's skill with plans cannot become civilian coercion. Greg's jokes cannot cancel his real disappointment or grief about Rose.

A sensual scene can linger on warmth, closeness, an awkward interruption or a mutually requested kiss, followed naturally by a private closed door. Do not include explicit sex, the first-person narrator describing everybody else's private life, forced exclusivity, or pressure on partners to date one another.
