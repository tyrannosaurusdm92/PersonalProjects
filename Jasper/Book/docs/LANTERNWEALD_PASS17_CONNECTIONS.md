# Lanternweald Pass 17 — A World of Return Routes, Repaired Things and Chosen Company

**Continuity supplement, November 6, 2026.** This is a writer-facing document supporting the six existing novels. The first sections summarize facts **actually written** in Pass 17; the final sections clearly separate proposals **not yet present in a chapter**. Do not silently retcon established history or claim this document has added unplayed events to the novels.

## Foundational principle

Lanternweald should feel as richly inhabited as a character-driven life-simulation setting without copying another game's locations or mechanics. The valley has seven neighborhoods, but it also has clocks, weather, wages, damaged equipment, people who know one another, arguments that linger, and reasons for leaving. It isn't a magical mall where every famous visitor comes for Jasper's affection.

A Threadway crossing requires invitation and choice. It never erases consequences in the origin world. No visitor automatically gets the archive key, the best ship, a volunteer's labor, exclusive romantic access or a new job. The mysterious lower Closed Bells flood passage is **still sealed**. The historical keeper's name is **still unverified**. A safer surface channel and a navigable harbor are not the same structure as that passage.

## Character-to-district causal threads now on the page

### Tideglass Cove — where pride must learn the tide

**Maui 22:** Fen's dated salinity readings, planting committee authority and two jars of soil expose the difference between a demigod's claim and a gardener's knowledge. Iroh's instinct to rearrange a garden is different from Maui's instinct to announce a legendary feat. Fen corrects both.

**Maui 43:** Edda presents a narrow turn and unworkable emergency exit. Jasper's preferred exploration vote loses. Maui's strength cannot be the emergency evacuation plan. An alternative costed surface route can be researched later, but the sealed lower passage does not open.

**Maui 49:** Moana sends a route chart and asks for comparison readings. Her own crew will decide whether the next voyage proceeds. Lio's transport and Fen's date-specific measurements connect the separate seas without claiming they have identical currents. Maui may help and brag, but he cannot captain her independent life.

The harbor therefore has three layered forms of authority: navigation by the captain and crew, local measurements by Fen and other coastal workers, and access/load decisions by the people who test the landing. Jasper can carry information between groups but does not replace them.

### Lanternwell Square — who writes the public version?

**Maui 15:** A visitor mistakes Jasper for a reward earned by Maui. Mara is still selling bread, Greg is thinking about his guitar, and Iroh is assessing the room. Their reactions are different. Jasper explicitly refuses to let the feast decide the meaning of their relationships.

An important ongoing distinction: public town events can discuss schedules, fees, safety, available work, and consent to photographs or speeches. They do not automatically acquire romantic disclosures, private routes or a visitor's history. Mara can charge for bread; Pip can charge for signage. Neither is an infinite free quest machine.

### Jasmine Steps and Ba Sing Se — clocks in a teahouse

**Iroh 26:** Mina can end a shift and require advance bookings instead of rearranging other people's lives around the former general's generosity. Iroh's shame, political obligations and business instincts coexist.

**Iroh 46 and Severus 42:** The quiet conflict is not who can deliver the best maxim. Iroh respects a declined invitation, while Severus resents being deprived of an argument. A White Lotus letter and shop inventory both have legitimate claims on Iroh's time. Tea service has its own wages, cups and returnable equipment.

The Jasmine Dragon remains **in Ba Sing Se**, not transplanted to the Lanternweald's Jasmine Steps. Visiting does not make Mina a Lanternweald citizen or an unpaid helper.

### Starwake Pier and Beach City — a venue isn't a favor

**Greg 22:** A microphone lead fails. The technician can stop use and require a tag and replacement. The organizer can change the set only through a real agreement with the performer. Greg's nervous humor and rhythm are not Iroh's quiet strategy.

**Greg 43:** Adult Steven brings a casserole, talks privately with Jasper and makes clear he has a life to live apart from his father's new partnership. His adult status is a chosen future fanfiction extension of **Steven Universe Future**, not an official new canon episode. Steven's curiosity about the Threadways does not grant permission to cross one.

A performance at Starwake requires a booked timeslot, sound equipment, accessible approach, weather clearance and an agreed load-out crew. A performance in Beach City has its own organizers and schedule. When Greg goes home, his car wash and family life keep happening.

### Brasswatch Yard and Diamond City — who owns the work order?

**Danse 22:** Nick Valentine's detective work has a client, an invoice, and a right to protect a witness. Danse's official security instincts are not sufficient to compel disclosure. Nick remains witty, professional, and independent, rather than conveniently fixing Danse's prejudices.

**Danse 43:** Renn's component rating and the refugee's privacy govern the order. A damaged regulator cannot be made safe with heroism or a false certificate. Danse's transformation is gradual and practical: first, he must learn how to work without calling every disagreement insubordination.

Brasswatch jobs carry a named repairer, a separate operator, test records, posted rates, and a way to refuse an unsafe task. A person who has worn a uniform or lost one does not have to surrender their history to buy parts.

### Moonroot Archive — the ledger can say no

**Severus 15:** Poppy Pomfrey's question about risk records makes Snape preserve an old experimental note as historical evidence while attaching a modern safety addendum. Her authority is clinical and independent of Severus's reputation. This does not magically reverse his treatment of students.

**Severus 42:** A tea invitation can be declined without turning the evening into a romantic loyalty test. In Moonroot, Neri still controls public-record access; Iroh is visiting with borrowed cups and his own obligations.

The archive's independent rule is *provenance before convenience*. A correction shows when and why it was made. A damaged document is not evidence of the one missing answer the town wants.

### Riverstone Terrace and the Southern Water Tribe — the ground counts

**Love & Duty 28:** Toph detects a birthday plan from floor vibrations. Her preferences cannot be overridden by affectionate spectacle. Katara makes a concrete choice to ask; Aang learns the difference between a surprise and a violation.

**Love & Duty 41:** Beren's cart needs an actual turning point. Toph detects a shifting support. Katara asks about injuries and service continuity. Aang must confront a bridge as lived infrastructure rather than ceremonial accomplishment. Jasper records the clerk's actual message.

People outside Lanternweald's boundaries continue to matter. The South Pole's clinicians determine its own requested supplies. Cranefish Town's delivery workers and engineers have jobs that need doing whether the protagonists are in love or not.

## Seven districts, six novels, one web of unglamorous recurring jobs

To keep each district distinct, scenes should use the following *ordinary obligations*, not interchangeable magical quests:

| District | Repeatable local work | Who can disagree | Consequence if neglected |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lanternwell Square | bread prices, public notices, optional volunteering | Mara, Pip, Neri | rumors crowd out safety notices; food and supplies go unaccounted |
| Tideglass Cove | dated tide marks, salinity samples, mooring checks | Fen, Lio, crews | incorrect route windows and damage to coastal beds |
| Jasmine Steps | cup returns, paid tea tastings, stall cleaning | shop owners, workers | lost inventory and unpaid shifts |
| Riverstone Terrace | bridge tests, loads, clinic access, route clearances | Edda, Beren, Katara, Toph | carts miss deliveries; inaccessible exits stay invisible |
| Moonroot Archive | source annotation, confidential corrections, bench upkeep | Orin, Neri, readers | names leak or errors become permanent public claims |
| Brasswatch Yard | load tests, lock access, real electrical ratings | Renn, Ash, Danse | unsafe tools and exclusion from work |
| Starwake Pier | sound checks, stage access, booked sets, crew load-out | Nola, Tamsin, Greg | unsafe shows and unpaid labor |

These are **recurring story systems**, not a claim that a particular daily mini-game already exists in the repository.

## Planned narrative seeds — explicitly NOT yet chapter canon

1. **Two maps, three users:** Beren, Edda and a harbor porter test the new Riverstone-to-Tideglass transfer route with different loads. The maps initially disagree; the volunteers can refuse a poorly scheduled test.
2. **Festival accounts:** Mara's bakery receipts, Pip's signage invoice and Nola's crew sheet become the same budget problem without making any of them unpaid helpers.
3. **Moana's return reply:** A later chart reaches Maui with corrected bearings and an independent decision by Moana's crew. If the chapter includes a visit, Moana's decision to enter the Threadway remains explicit.
4. **Beach City interruption:** Greg has to choose whether he can keep both a Starwake rehearsal and a real appointment with Steven. The solution is a changed schedule, not a miracle or a compulsory family speech.
5. **Archives under review:** Pomfrey and Orin compare different kinds of evidence about historical potions. Neither may publish a confidential patient statement or claim an old experimental margin note is a safe clinical method.
6. **The right to decline:** A resident refuses to become an exhibit in the town's new character archive. Their refusal remains a full scene, not a problem Jasper solves by persuasion.
7. **The storm that actually closes the pier:** Tamsin shuts the stage; Nola decides whether and where a paid indoor event can be held. The performer has a right to say no.
8. **Winter route testing:** Fen's coast readings and Edda's turning data disagree about a proposed delivery path. The discrepancy is recorded, paid workers test it, and the original sealed passage remains closed.

## Romance and Jasper's narration

Jasper is an adult, first-person protagonist. The explicit relationship ethic already established in the books is **ethical non-monogamy with a preference for polyamory**, discussed openly with partners but not announced as a public scheduling right. Distinct partners have different needs, work, histories and ways of asking for reassurance. Sensuality remains non-explicit and may end with a natural fade to black after chosen intimacy; it never substitutes for a conversation that needs to happen.

Use the previously recorded prose-style corpus: concrete sensory details, imperfect humor, short charged responses, longer introspection when earned. Avoid making every moment a speech about consent, a social-service meeting, or a claim that all adults are infinitely available.
