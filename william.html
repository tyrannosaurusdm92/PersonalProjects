Hi, I'm William

𓃑

I make things I wish existed.

Stories. Games. Tools. Very large fictional worlds. Occasionally a cat with far too much authority.

I am William Saville, the person behind The Transgender T-Rex. I write, build games, design TTRPG ideas, make little digital tools, and keep creating worlds that get more complicated every time I touch them.

Most of my projects do not start with a business plan. They start with a need, a question, or a sentence like, “why does this not already exist?” Sometimes Jasper and I need something for our own life. Sometimes a game mechanic will not leave me alone. Sometimes I write one paragraph of lore and suddenly I have three moons, four continents, a language history, and several thousand years of political problems.

Right now: I am building OurSpace and Onyx for the life Jasper and I actually live, developing Aza'ra across books and games, making mobile and tabletop-inspired games, and writing whatever refuses to stay quiet in my head.

Things that keep showing up in my work

✦
I care whether people feel remembered. That shows up in everything from pronouns and accessibility to saved game history and the way I build spaces for Jasper and me.
I hate when a world forgets its own past. If something happened, I want it to leave fingerprints. On characters, families, buildings, politics, language, or the player.
I take small details seriously. If a character can fly, flying should actually matter. If a button says it saves something, it should save it. If Onyx is sitting on the couch, he should look like he belongs on the couch.
I also think fun is a good enough reason. Not everything has to heal somebody or prove something. Sometimes a game is fun, a cat is ridiculous, and that is the whole point.

The short version

✦

I am William.

I am a trans man, a disabled creator, Jasper's fiancé, a cat parent, and somebody who has never been especially good at doing anything halfway.

I am stubborn, curious, emotional, creative, sometimes exhausted, and very capable of turning a tiny idea into a project with lore, folders, rules, and a roadmap before I notice what happened.

I do not have a neat career label. Writer fits. Worldbuilder fits. Game maker fits. Programmer fits. Designer fits. Advocate fits sometimes too. Mostly, I am a person who notices things, cares a lot, and tends to answer “I wish this worked differently” by trying to build a different version.

I am not interested in making my life sound inspirational. I am proud of what I have survived and what I have made, but I still wish some of the surviving had never been necessary.

A lot happened before I got here

✦

I have lived through abuse, homelessness, violence, exploitation, trafficking, disability, grief, and long stretches where stability felt temporary. I do not put that here as a list of accomplishments. It is simply part of the context for why certain things matter so much to me.

I notice what other people miss

I learned young to read rooms, tone, tension, body language, and the little shifts that tell you something is wrong. Sometimes that becomes hypervigilance. Sometimes it means I notice the person who is uncomfortable before anybody asks if they are okay.

I have a hard time looking away

If I know somebody is being hurt or treated unfairly, silence is difficult for me. That can be a good quality and a reckless one. I am still learning that doing the right thing does not require treating myself as disposable.

My family is complicated, because families are

✦

I miss my dad. A lot. I can miss him, remember the parts of him I loved, and still be honest that he hurt me. One of the deepest wounds in that relationship was feeling like he looked at me and saw my biological mother instead of seeing me.

My relationship with my stepmom has changed in a way I did not expect. She was one of the main reasons I wrote Eggshells. After my dad died, she took accountability for abuse and neglect from my childhood, and we started rebuilding something I genuinely did not think we were going to have.

That has taught me more about accountability than any clean quote ever could. It does not erase the old relationship. It gives the current one a chance to be different.

Brick by stubborn brick. That line from Little Moments is probably the closest thing I have to a summary of how I understand healing now.

Disability changes the size of a day

✦

I have dealt with homelessness since 2010, and I live with disabilities that change the logistics of almost everything. Housing is not just rent. It is whether a wheelchair fits. Whether a bathroom is usable. Whether equipment has power. Whether transportation works. Whether one bad physical day turns the rest of the week sideways.

My brain does not always agree with the limits of my body. I can have ten things I want to make on a day when my body is voting for zero. Digital work gives me somewhere to move anyway. I can write a chapter from a chair. I can build a room I cannot physically walk through. I can make a world enormous on a day when my own world feels very small.

That is also why I care so much about things not disappearing. I like backups. I like continuity. I like knowing where the current version is. I have had enough instability in real life. I do not need my fictional world or my save file deciding to join in.

Where I am now

✦

I am still learning how to care deeply without making myself the price of caring. My first instinct is usually to fix the problem, research the answer, build the thing, protect the person, or stay up until I can make something work.

Sometimes that instinct is useful. Sometimes I need to stop.

I am, I am, enough. I wrote that before I fully knew how much I needed to believe it. I am not finished, and I do not need to be finished to deserve a life that is mine.

More Precious Than Diamonds

January 6, 2024
A poem for my love Jasper Faye Szimanski

When their smile is like sunshine,
When your sky is full of storms,
When they whisper words of love,
My ice wall slowly warms.

Protected my heart from endless trauma,
With these walls of ice I built,
Instead of breaking my walls down,
They warm them instead of guilt.

Each day we live on halted breath,
Hand in hand we take the days
Waiting for the other shoe to drop,
Taking solace in each other's gaze.

Traumas are talked about,
Rather than avoided or buried,
Making us better partners,
Knowing each other's hearts are carried.

Pave the road ahead of us,
With each moment and heartbeat,
More precious than diamonds or gold,
No decadent dessert could be as sweet.

So my love and my partner,
They are the fire inside me,
Pushing me to do more than survive,
I want to be more than happy.

With them I can have a life,
Worth living hand in hand
I can thrive in their embrace,
No matter what we withstand.

What love means to me

✦

I am polyamorous. I do not think love becomes less meaningful because it is not exclusive. I care about honesty, consent, communication, respect, and whether the people in a relationship are being treated like full human beings instead of property.

One thing I keep learning: protection and control are not the same thing. I can be fiercely protective when I love someone, but fear does not give me the right to make somebody else's choices for them.

OurSpace started with us

✦

OurSpace exists because Jasper and I kept needing things that lived in completely different apps, tabs, notebooks, reminders, and conversations. I wanted one private place built around the way we actually live.

I usually write when something will not leave me alone.

Sometimes it is grief. Sometimes anger. Sometimes love. Sometimes a character shows up with a problem and refuses to go away until I give them a world.

Writing is also how I find out what I think. I can spend months talking around something and then write one line in a poem that makes me realize I understood it before I knew I understood it.

What I keep writing about, whether I mean to or not

✦
People being told who they are. A lot of my characters eventually have to decide whether they are going to keep living inside somebody else's version of them.
Chosen family. I write a lot of people building home out of the people who stay.
Bodily autonomy. Gods, parents, governments, doctors, lovers, and heroes can all convince themselves they know what is best for somebody else. Power does not make that automatically true.
Protection becoming control. This one shows up constantly. Probably because I know how easy it is for fear and love to get tangled together.
What happens after survival. I care less about trauma making somebody magically strong than I do about what they choose once the immediate danger is over.

✦

I am very bad at leaving a good idea small

⚙

Most of my projects begin with “this could be simple.”

Then I start asking questions.

What happens if somebody comes back tomorrow? What if they are playing alone? What if they use a wheelchair? What if the app gets their name wrong? What if they lose progress? What if the NPC remembers what happened three chapters ago? What if the cat is hungry?

That is usually the point where the simple project stops being simple.

I am not adding detail because I think bigger automatically means better. I add it when the detail makes the thing feel more alive, more useful, more personal, or more honest.

Poems & Short Stories

How tabletop games got me here

🎲

I have been carrying tabletop ideas around for roughly a decade. GMing gave me one place where writing, rules, maps, characters, history, improv, and other people's completely unpredictable decisions all had to work together.

That is where a lot of my design habits came from. I do not just want a rule to sound good on paper. I want to know what it feels like when somebody actually uses it. If you can fly, I want flight to matter. If an NPC has a life, I want the world to remember it. If a player spends an hour making a character, I want the game to respect that investment tomorrow too.

Eventually “house rules” turned into custom classes, encounter tools, movement systems, worldbuilding tools, persistent campaigns, and several projects that are now their own thing.

Aza'ra

☾

Aza'ra is the world I keep coming home to.

Threads of Fate • New Age • Dakaroth • Arzuhan • Tzaphaer • Belavadös

I build Aza'ra around the things that make a world feel lived in: the people who remember it, the stories that get passed down, the rituals that outlast their beginnings, and the physical details of a place that shape everybody living there.

The books and the MMORPG live in different eras of the same history. I love that because it means something a player experiences as ordinary life in New Age can survive centuries later as a religion, a ruin, a political argument, a family story, or a completely wrong version of what really happened.

The thing I keep coming back to: history and memory are not the same thing.

A world beneath the Veil

✦

Aza'ra is a shattered, disc-shaped world suspended in the Void. Its four separate continents, Dakaroth, Arzuhan, Tzaphaer, and Belavadös, share a single magical enclosure. Islands drift above the land; beneath the surface, the broken world narrows into a twisting, fractured cone.

The Veil curves over the surface as a mostly transparent dome. Nothing passes through it, including clouds and water. Outside it, there is no surviving life. Inside it, every storm, coastline, settlement, journey, and act of magic belongs to an enclosed world whose sky has a limit.

Its sun is dead or almost dead. The light people actually live by comes from the Veil and the three moons it illuminates. That gives the sky its own rhythms instead of simply borrowing Earth's day and night.

Two pieces of Aza'ra I made for Jasper

♥

Arvalla, the night moon

I named and designed Arvalla for Jasper. I made it a vast blue-silver moon with rings tilted roughly 35 degrees and storms that never truly settle. It belongs to the La hours of the Aza'raan day, the nighttime stretch when the world is under its influence.

Arvalla is tied to mystery, dreams, transformation, introspection, and psionic or divine communion. Those details are deliberate. When I look at Aza'ra's sky, I want Jasper's place in the world to feel as permanent as the moon itself.

The Emberchild

The Emberchild is also for Jasper. I designed the constellation as a trail of ember-bright stars reaching toward a flame, with a small paired spark beside it. Its linework glows warm orange, and its stars shine a softer gold.

Its story is about a mortal child who steals fire from a forge and is given a divine spark. It carries themes of ingenuity, childhood, fire, and becoming. The Emberchild is especially visible in Aza'ra's seventh month, around midday, from Tzaphaer.

They are two very different images, a storm-lit blue moon and a reaching line of fire, but both are part of how I have written Jasper into Aza'ra's heavens.

Hours, months, and moonlight

☾☾☾

Aza'raan time is measured in 21 hours per day, six days per week, five weeks per month, and eight months per 240-day year. The three moon periods are Ae, W’i, and La, with seven hours in each.

Duvaen is the red moon associated with beginnings and vitality. W'iosamn is the silver moon of clarity and civic order. Arvalla is the blue, ringed night moon. Their cycles affect how I think about the world's rituals and everyday life, not just what a person sees by looking up.

The Solar System and Constellations pages let visitors explore these systems: the moons and their paths, other worlds and the nebulae, the Veil clock, stars, sky signs, and the named constellations.

There is an entire sky beyond the Veil

✧

The surrounding atlas includes rocky worlds, gas giants, distant ice planets, and drifting material belts. Worlds like Surixoch-Aresu and Enlimitra give Aza'ra a place in a wider system rather than leaving it alone against a painted backdrop.

Eight named nebulae have identities of their own, including Âhufej, the Minotaur Labyrinth of Anubaldir, and Dus‘idu, the Silkborn Web of Thoryn-Rahek. Each has its own textures, colors, and layered cloud formations.

Even the comet has a name: Eiršu'neth, an icy pale blue-white and smoky-silver wanderer whose long orbit takes about 6.48 Aza'raan years. I like when the biggest things in the sky are still specific enough to have paths, patterns, and names that matter to the people beneath them.

Before Aza'ra was Aza'ra

☾☾☾

Before the Cataclysm, the world was Uxrowo. The Uxrowoiye were a psionic people whose lives were tied closely to the land, their rituals, and three moons that did much more than look pretty in the sky.

Duvaen was crimson and tied to beginnings, movement, vitality, and work. W'iosamn was pale silver and tied to clarity, civic life, councils, and order. Arvalla was blue-silver and ringed, tied to dreams, mystery, transformation, and deeper psionic practice.

This is the kind of worldbuilding rabbit hole I love. I do not just want three moons. I want to know what three moons do to schedules, rituals, language, art, government, and the way somebody understands what time of day even means.

The day the world broke

✦

In 1351 BCE, a dying alien civilization tried to save itself by draining Uxrowo's sun. It destroyed the sun instead, and Uxrowo broke with it.

Makana, Kalino, and Salamasina, three Protopolynesian magicians from Fenua, the world later known as Earth, had arrived shortly before the disaster. Their magic met Uxrowoiye psionics at exactly the moment both were needed.

Hundreds of portals opened. The Veil was woven. Broken landmasses were stabilized in the Void. The moons survived. Enough people survived to build something afterward.

The world was renamed Azah, savior in Uxrowoiye, and across the enormous stretch of history that followed, Azah became Aza'ra.

I want cultures to leave fingerprints

✦

Aza'ra is not built around one people arriving and replacing everybody else. Uxrowoiye survivors, the families of the Protopolynesian rescuers, and other magic-sensitive people from Fenua become part of what later turns into Aza'raan civilization.

I want that history visible in ordinary things. In names. In language. In writing systems. In who gets remembered as a founder and who gets reduced to a footnote.

On Arzuhan, Makana and King Akage establish the Royal Bloodline. Kalino founds the Arbiter Bloodline. Salamasina founds the Protector Bloodline. The Key of Kings exists because I am much more interested in the question “what makes somebody legitimate?” than I am in handing a protagonist a crown and calling that the ending.

Pix and Threads of Fate

✦

In 2036, Pix is a trans man and wheelchair user living on Earth with a found family made out of people who learned to survive together. Then a hidden portal drops them into Aza'ra, and Pix learns the world is not as foreign to him as he thought.

Aza'ra is his birthplace. His father died in the crash that changed his family. His mother survived, but eventually needed assisted living and could not provide the level of care Pix needed, so he entered foster care. His psychic grandmother had given a prophecy about a son. His parents thought the danger had passed because they did not understand that the son in the prophecy was already there.

Pix also begins the story grieving a brother believed dead before the first page. In Aza'ra, he learns that his bloodline and his role as Arbiter tie him to a throne, a false ruler, rebellion, broken portals, and a history people have been manipulating for a very long time.

I do not want Pix to be “the disabled character” or “the trans character.” He gets to be funny, angry, loving, wrong, powerful, petty, frightened, complicated, and occasionally a pain in the ass. Like a person.

The gods are not who Earth thinks they are

✦

Aza'ra's divine beings have their own names. Zeus, Persephone, Hera, and the other familiar names are Earth labels, not their Aza'raan identities. Earth remembers pieces of them through myth, and those memories can be distorted, political, incomplete, or simply wrong.

When I need to make the connection, I use the form known on Earth as <deity name>.

A theme I keep coming back to: being powerful does not make somebody right. Creating a person does not mean owning them. Loving somebody does not mean owning them either.

Aza'ra: New Age

✦

New Age is the MMORPG side of Aza'ra, set far earlier than Threads of Fate.

I wanted the history of the books to be somewhere people could actually stand. A player can live through an event that a character centuries later only knows through a damaged archive, a family story, a religious tradition, or propaganda. That gap between what happened and what survived is one of my favorite things to play with.

Old things make better worlds

✦

That is probably why I struggle to make “just a setting.” I want to know what used to be where the building stands, why the road bends, which word came from another language, who changed the story, and what everybody has been misunderstanding for three hundred years.
